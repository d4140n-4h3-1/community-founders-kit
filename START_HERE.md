# Start Here: Your First Season

This page is for a founding group of 5-10 people at **Tier 0**: no money, no land of your own, and no experience yet. The full handbook covers everything up to owning land and running a village. You do not need most of it yet. This page lists what you need now, in order, and what you can safely leave for later. To see where it all leads, read [What Success Looks Like](handbook/part-4-tiers/what-success-looks-like.md).

## Week 1

- [ ] Gather the founding group of 5-10 committed people
- [ ] Hold the first meeting, using the [first-meeting agenda](handbook/appendices/appendix-b-templates/b02-first-meeting-agenda.md)
- [ ] Write a one-paragraph purpose and three to five shared values
- [ ] Have the honest conversation: time, money, housing, children, substances and weapons, beliefs, privacy, decisions, conflict, leaving, and failure
- [ ] Name roles, with a backup for each: cash, keys, filings, safeguarding, first aid, crops, spokesperson, and minutes
- [ ] Ask a trusted non-resident to be the group's **outside advisor**, who joins decisions on the reserve and hears appeals until the community has a board ([Chapter 3](handbook/part-1-core-chapters/03-charter-and-decision-making.md#who-acts-as-the-board-before-tier-3))
- [ ] Open a community bank account with two signers
- [ ] Book time with an accountant and an attorney, and ask the market manager about vendor rules
- [ ] Set the next three actions, with names and dates

## The First 90 Days

- **Weeks 1-2:** draft the [founding agreement](handbook/appendices/appendix-b-templates/b01-founding-agreement.md), and start the [contribution and volunteer-hours log](handbook/appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d07-contribution-and-volunteer-hours-log.md) and the [expense log](handbook/appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d06-expense-log.md)
- **Weeks 3-6:** see the accountant and the attorney, check local rules, and start the [compliance calendar](handbook/appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d09-compliance-calendar.md)
- **Weeks 6-10:** talk with possible landowners or hosts, get any land permission in a written [land-use agreement](handbook/appendices/appendix-b-templates/b06-land-use-agreement.md), and start a small garden or container beds
- **Weeks 10-13:** review together, adjust the agreement, and decide whether to continue, adjust, or pause

## What Tier 0 Costs, and How to Pay for It

"No funds" does not mean no costs. A few things must be paid for before the first market sale brings any money in. Get quotes early, put them in the first one-page budget, and pay for them from recorded voluntary contributions and small grants.

| Cost | Why it can't wait | Lower-cost routes |
|---|---|---|
| Attorney review of the founding and land-use agreements | Required before Tier 1, and before any money or labor goes into the land | Legal aid or law-school clinics, nonprofit legal services, a host organization's attorney, or a flat-fee review |
| Accountant consultation | Before the first sale or regular contribution | Volunteer tax-help programs, a small-business advice center, or a fiscal sponsor's bookkeeper |
| Liability insurance | Before anyone sleeps on the land, hosts visitors, or sells | A host organization's or fiscal sponsor's policy, a market's group policy, and quotes from several brokers |
| Drinking-water test | Before anyone drinks it | Health department or extension-service lab rates |
| Soil test | Before planting food | Extension-service soil test kits, which are usually low cost |
| First-aid training | One trained first-aider before move-in | Community first-aid courses, often discounted for volunteers |
| Vendor permit and market fees | Before the first market day | Ask the market about new-vendor or shared-stall rates |
| Toilet and handwashing | From the first day on the land | Share a host's facilities, or split a serviced portable toilet |
| Fire extinguisher, smoke and CO detectors, and a first-aid kit | Before anyone sleeps on the land | Donations of new units (never used detectors) |

Partnering with an existing organization ([Chapter 2](handbook/part-1-core-chapters/02-forming-a-group.md)) can cover insurance, banking, and legal review at once. If the group cannot yet cover these costs, it is not ready to put people on the land. Keep meeting, saving, and growing in containers until it is.

## Before Anyone Sleeps on the Land

Work through the [site readiness checklist](handbook/appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a01-site-readiness.md). The most important items:

- [ ] Written land permission, and local approvals in writing
- [ ] Tested drinking water, in sealed containers, and a legal toilet with a handwashing station
- [ ] Everyone knows the water rule: any sign of tampering means [don't drink, test first](handbook/part-3-site-and-infrastructure/10-water-plumbing-and-septic/05-safety-maintenance-and-failures.md#protecting-the-water-supply)
- [ ] Tents on platforms, anchored, away from hazards
- [ ] A first-aid kit, a trained first-aider, and emergency numbers, the site address, and directions posted
- [ ] A fire extinguisher, and smoke and CO detectors where people sleep
- [ ] Food in locked, rodent-proof containers
- [ ] Any firearms locked in a case cabled to something solid, in a locked vehicle safe, or kept off site ([Firearms Safety](handbook/part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/05-firearms-safety.md))
- [ ] Insurance in place for what you plan to do
- [ ] An access road an ambulance can use, and [two ways out](handbook/part-3-site-and-infrastructure/12-roads-and-access.md#two-ways-out) identified
- [ ] How long an ambulance takes to arrive, and a way to call for help where there's no cell signal ([Remote Medical Plan](handbook/part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/04-health-leadership-and-leaving.md#remote-medical-plan))

## Daily Life From Day One

Keep it simple and fair from the start ([Daily Life](handbook/part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/08-daily-life-chores-shared-spaces-and-routines.md)):

- A chore list and a [weekly chore chart](handbook/appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a11-weekly-chore-chart.md), with essential tasks covered every day
- About 12 hours of work a week per adult, as a guideline, never a condition of housing
- Quiet hours, shared-space rules, and one rest day a week
- Boundary signs, a visitor sign-in, one named spokesperson, and the [outside incident log](handbook/appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d16-outside-incident-and-harassment-log.md)

## Money From Day One

- A one-page [budget](handbook/appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d05-monthly-budget.md), an expense log, and a receipt for everything
- No market sale until the accountant has been consulted, insurance is in place, and sales tax is registered if it applies
- Two people count the cash at the start and end of every market day ([cash-count sheet](handbook/appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d02-cash-count-sheet.md)), and every sale goes in the [sales log](handbook/appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d01-sales-log.md)

## Planning for a Year, Not a Month

If your market closes for part of the year, plan those months in the budget from the start. The market season has to earn enough to carry the quiet months, and a planned off-season is never a reason to step back a tier ([Chapter 8](handbook/part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/01-budgeting-cash-handling-and-cost-tracking.md)).

## What You Can Skip for Now

These matter later, and the handbook covers them, but they are not Tier 0 work:

- Cameras and the meeting recording archive (Tier 2-3)
- Cabins, the food store, septic installation, and greenhouses (Tier 2 and up)
- The Lodge and the Meeting Hall (planned at Tier 2, built at Tier 3)
- Forming a nonprofit or land trust, and resident fees (Tier 3)
- Reserve layers beyond the cash reserve, precious metals, and buying land (Tier 3-4)
- Keeping your own animals, wind turbines, and the sand battery (Tier 3 and up). A hired grazing service or a local beekeeper's hosted hives can come sooner, under a written agreement ([Animals](handbook/part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/09-animals.md)).

## How You Know You're Ready for Tier 1

When every item in the [Tier 0 → Tier 1 unlock trigger](handbook/part-4-tiers/unlock-triggers/01-tier-0-tier-1.md) is met, hold a vote. Use the [tier review worksheet](handbook/appendices/appendix-e-tier-review-worksheet.md), and record the date, the vote, and what you learned in the revision log. Staying at Tier 0 longer is normal, not failure.
