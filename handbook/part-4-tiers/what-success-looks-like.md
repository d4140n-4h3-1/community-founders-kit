# What Success Looks Like

This is a picture of a community that has reached [Tier 4](tier-summaries/05-tier-4-own.md) and met the [graduation](unlock-triggers/05-tier-4-graduation.md) conditions. It is a goal to steer by, not a promise. Most groups take years to get here, many stay longer at a tier along the way, and **staying at a tier is normal, not failure.**

## In One Sentence

**A village of about 20-30 people on land it owns, paying its own way from the market, safe and fair to everyone who lives there, and teaching the next group how to do the same.**

## The Place

- **Homes:** six to eight family cabins plus a few smaller ones. Each has a steel or filled-block shell, a fire-rated interior, at most two stories, and room for no more than four people ([Shelter Evolution](../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/09-shelter-evolution/README.md)). Every porch has an amber bug light, and the garden spiders are left alone.
- **Shared buildings:** a **Lodge** for meals, celebrations, and classes, booked equally by everyone, with no cameras, and a separate **Meeting Hall** where open meetings are recorded for the community's archive. A filled-block **food store** is stocked from the community's own harvests.
- **Growing:** market plots, greenhouses with one heated for year-round seedlings, fruit trees and berries that are finally bearing, and, if the group voted for them, livestock with a written predator plan.
- **Water and land:** a sealed, locked water supply, tested and checked daily, an inspected septic system, gravel roads an ambulance can use, and **two ways out** ([Chapter 12](../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/12-roads-and-access.md#two-ways-out)). The growing and storage areas may be fenced, with barbed wire only where it guards key resources.
- **Energy:** LED lighting everywhere, solar tubes in the roofs, and solar power. There may be a small wind turbine and a sand battery, but only if a year of records justified them.
- **Network** (if adopted): fiber between buildings, FreeBSD servers on low-power ARM boards in a locked cabinet, and a public computer in the Lodge ([Digital Infrastructure](../bonus/digital-infrastructure.md)).

## How It Runs

- **Money:** income from at least two sales outlets pays the bills, and no single outlet brings in most of it. A reserve covers six months of costs, a separate fund holds a full year's property tax, and the books are independently reviewed ([Chapter 8](../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md)).
- **Ownership:** the land belongs to a nonprofit or land trust whose board includes people who don't live there. No individual holds the title.
- **Records:** every tax, fee, and filing is current, and an annual report shows anyone where the money goes.
- **Two people for every critical job:** cash, keys, filings, safeguarding, first aid, the archive, the network, and each main crop.
- **Neighbors:** the community is a known, ordinary neighbor. It pays its taxes, buys locally, donates surplus food, holds open days, and answers complaints with its records.

## How It Feels to Live There

- About **12 hours of shared work a week** per adult, fairly split and adjusted in writing, with care work counted, and **a rest day every week** ([Daily Life](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/08-daily-life-chores-shared-spaces-and-routines.md))
- Shared meals, harvest celebrations, and arrivals and farewells marked together
- Conflicts settled through the written ladder, usually at the first step, and people who leave doing so on good terms
- Everyone treated equally, whatever they believe, with the halls open to all on the same terms
- Each household with its own door, key, and privacy, and no camera pointed at anyone's home

## How It Stays Safe

- **Safeguarding** that reaches everyone, including leaders, with concerns passed to the right authority and never hidden ([Safeguarding](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/02-safeguarding-children-and-vulnerable-adults.md))
- **A remote medical plan** sized to how long help takes to arrive, with trained first-aiders, an AED, and a helicopter landing spot ([Remote Medical Plan](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/04-health-leadership-and-leaving.md#remote-medical-plan))
- **Firearms** locked up, carried concealed or not at all, and farm guns used only by trained adults ([Firearms Safety](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/05-firearms-safety.md))
- **Locks, lights, and cameras** on the places worth protecting, every key and code audited each year, and a calm, documented response to outside pressure ([Outside Harassment and Community Security](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/06-outside-harassment-and-community-security.md))
- **Stable governance:** big decisions need a supermajority and a cooling-off period, and members vote on them after a year of membership, so no group can take over quickly

## The Signs It Has Succeeded

These are the [graduation](unlock-triggers/05-tier-4-graduation.md) conditions, plus the signs that it will last:

- [ ] The community **owns its land,** and the property tax fund holds a full year's bill before any resident fee starts
- [ ] It has **mentored at least one new group** from Tier 0, such as a [sister village](../bonus/sister-villages.md)
- [ ] It has **written its own lessons back into the handbook,** so the next group starts better prepared
- [ ] **Leadership has passed** from the founders to newer members, and the community would carry on if any one person left
- [ ] **Turnover is steady and leaving is friendly:** most people who leave do so on good terms, and say why
- [ ] **Two or more full seasons** of reviewed records, and annual reports published each year

## What Success Is Not

- **Not a town, unless the community chooses one.** The handbook is written for a village. Growing larger is a legitimate choice when every resource allows it ([Sister Villages](../bonus/sister-villages.md#growing-larger-or-starting-a-sister-village)).
- **Not wealth.** The goal is a community that pays its way and keeps a reserve, not one that makes anyone rich.
- **Not a nation.** The community, and any federation it joins, stays openly lawful ([Federation of Villages](../bonus/federation-of-villages.md)).
- **Not perfection.** Conflicts still happen, a bad season still comes, and a tier may still need to step back. Success is handling them well.
- **Not finished.** The community keeps reviewing its rules each season and each year, and keeps changing what doesn't work.

## Beyond One Village

A successful village often becomes the start of something larger: a [sister village](../bonus/sister-villages.md) for the people on its waiting list, then perhaps a [federation](../bonus/federation-of-villages.md) of villages sharing a market, a training school, mutual aid, and a land trust. Each village stays small enough to work the way this handbook describes.

> **The real measure.** The handbook counts a community as successful not when it is finished, but when it can carry on without its founders and help others begin.
