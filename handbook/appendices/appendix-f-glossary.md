# Appendix F: Glossary

Plain meanings for the words this handbook uses in its own way. Where a term has a full chapter, the link goes there.

## Growth and Governance

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tier** | One of five stages, from Tier 0 (no funds) to Tier 4 (owning land). Each tier sets what the community may build and spend on. See [Part 4](../part-4-tiers/README.md). |
| **Unlock trigger** | The checklist a community must fully meet, and then vote on, before moving to the next tier. See [Unlock Triggers](../part-4-tiers/unlock-triggers/README.md). |
| **Graduation** | The last set of conditions after Tier 4, including mentoring a new group. |
| **Stepping back** | Returning to the previous tier's scale when the cash balance stays below the budget plan for two months in a row, or a year's income does not cover that year's costs. A planned off-season is not a reason to step back. |
| **Founding agreement** | The first written agreement every founder signs ([B1](appendix-b-templates/b01-founding-agreement.md)). It becomes the first draft of the charter. |
| **Charter** | The community's governing document, holding its standing rules. It grows tier by tier ([Chapter 3](../part-1-core-chapters/03-charter-and-decision-making.md#when-each-rule-is-adopted)). |
| **Core charter** | The rules every group adopts before Tier 1: decisions, money, conduct, safeguarding, firearms, equal treatment, and dissolution. |
| **Board** | The body that makes the community's biggest decisions. Before Tier 3, the governance meeting plus the outside advisor; from Tier 3, the nonprofit or land trust board with non-resident members ([Chapter 3](../part-1-core-chapters/03-charter-and-decision-making.md#who-acts-as-the-board-before-tier-3)). |
| **Outside advisor** | A trusted non-resident named in the founding agreement who joins reserve decisions and hears appeals until the Tier 3 board exists. |
| **Standing rules** | The fixed rules listed in [Chapter 3](../part-1-core-chapters/03-charter-and-decision-making.md), which change only by the charter's amendment process. |
| **Supermajority** | A vote threshold above a simple majority, set in the charter, used for big decisions such as buying land. |
| **Cooling-off period** | A set wait between presenting a big decision and voting on it. |
| **Revision log** | The community's record of each tier vote, rule change, and lesson learned ([template](../../REVISION_LOG.md)). |

## People and Daily Life

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Conflict ladder** | The steps a dispute climbs, from a direct conversation up to mediation and a hearing ([B3](appendix-b-templates/b03-conduct-code-and-conflict-ladder.md)). |
| **Removal and appeal process** | The written steps for asking a member to leave, and for that member to appeal. |
| **Safeguarding lead** | The trained member, with a deputy, responsible for protecting children and vulnerable adults. |
| **Spokesperson** | The one named member who speaks for the community to outsiders, the press, and during harassment. |
| **Neighbor liaison** | The named member neighbors can contact with questions or complaints. |
| **Trial period** | A set time a newcomer lives and works with the community before full membership. |
| **Hours guideline** | About 12 hours of work a week per adult, adjusted fairly in writing. A guideline, never a condition of housing. |
| **Essential tasks** | Work that must be covered every day, including the rest day: water, toilets, food safety, greenhouse checks, and any animal care. |
| **Equal treatment** | The rule that no one is favored or excluded for what they believe, and no building is dedicated to a single belief. |

## Money

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Two-person cash count** | Two people count market cash together at the start and end of every market day ([D2](appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d02-cash-count-sheet.md)). |
| **Float** | The small amount of change taken to market at the start of the day. |
| **Break-even** | The income needed each month to cover operating costs. |
| **Operating reserve** | Cash, in insured accounts, set aside to cover months of operating costs. It is never converted into other assets. |
| **Store of value** | An asset held as a long-term hedge, such as precious metals. Allowed only from Tier 4, in a capped share, by board vote ([Chapter 8](../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/02-reserve-and-inflation-protection.md)). |
| **Capital plan** | The costed list of buildings and systems, in build order, with a funding source for each ([D20](appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d20-capital-plan-and-build-order.md)). |
| **Labor budget** | A weekly count of the hours the plan needs in the busiest weeks, compared with the hours members can give ([D21](appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d21-labor-budget.md)). |
| **Compliance calendar** | The list of every tax, fee, permit, and filing deadline, each with two responsible members ([D9](appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d09-compliance-calendar.md)). |
| **Annual report** | A yearly public summary of the community's work, money, and giving ([D10](appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d10-annual-report-template.md)). |
| **Community fee (resident fee)** | A monthly fee residents pay toward the property tax bill, only if the community owns land and has no tax exemption. |
| **Planned lot** | One household or cabin site, counted when setting the fee. Fees are divided by planned lots, not current residents. |
| **Property tax fund** | A dedicated account that holds fee money for the tax bill, and at least one full year's bill before any fee is due. |

## Land and Buildings

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Land-use agreement** | The written permission from a landowner for the group to use land it does not own ([B6](appendix-b-templates/b06-land-use-agreement.md)). |
| **Right of first refusal** | A clause giving the community the first chance to buy land if the owner sells ([B7](appendix-b-templates/b07-right-of-first-refusal.md)). |
| **Land trust** | A nonprofit that holds land for a community's long-term use. Title to community land is held by the nonprofit or land trust, never an individual. |
| **Due diligence** | The 12 checks done before buying land ([A7](appendix-a-checklists/a07-due-diligence-for-land.md)). |
| **Capacity check** | Confirming that people, money, water, septic, and roads can support more land before buying it. |
| **Percolation (perc) test** | A soil test that shows whether the ground can absorb septic water. |
| **Noncombustible shell** | A cabin's outer structure in steel or filled concrete block, so it does not burn. |
| **Fire-rated interior** | Drywall or another approved finish that slows the spread of fire inside a building. |
| **Egress** | A way out in an emergency, such as a door or a window large enough to climb through. |
| **Hoop house** | A simple, unheated greenhouse of hoops covered in plastic film. |
| **Wash-and-pack station** | The clean area where harvested produce is washed, cooled, and packed for market. |
| **Food store** | The filled-block building where the community's food is stored, with logged keys and monthly inspections. |
| **The Lodge** | The community's shared hall for social and general use, booked on equal terms. No cameras. |
| **The Meeting Hall** | The shared hall for governance meetings, where open meetings are recorded for the archive. |
| **Meeting archive** | The recordings of open meetings and their index ([D18](appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d18-meeting-archive-index.md)), kept by two archivists. |
| **Closed session** | A part of a meeting that is never recorded, such as a personnel or safeguarding matter. The minutes note the pause and the legal basis, not the content. |
| **Camera custodian** | One of the two named people allowed to view camera footage, with every viewing logged. |

## Energy, Growing, and Safety

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Solar tube** | A sealed tube that brings daylight through a roof into a room. |
| **Bug light** | An amber LED that attracts fewer insects, used on porches. |
| **Anemometer** | An instrument that measures wind speed. A year of its data comes before any turbine. |
| **Sand battery** | An insulated container of sand heated by surplus power that releases the heat later. It stores heat, not electricity ([Chapter 11](../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/11-sand-battery-heating.md)). |
| **Mini-split** | A ductless heat pump that heats and cools a room efficiently. |
| **Extension service** | A public agency (in the US, run with state universities) that gives local advice on soil, crops, and pests. Outside the US, look for the agricultural advisory service. |
| **Broad-spectrum pesticide** | A spray that kills many kinds of insects, including spiders and helpful ones. Not used in or near gardens. |
| **Farm gun** | A manual rifle or shotgun used only to protect livestock, handled only by trained, named adults. |

## Digital Infrastructure (Bonus)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Digital infrastructure** | The computers, servers, and network devices the community owns, runs, or offers for shared use. It runs Linux or a BSD ([Bonus](../bonus/digital-infrastructure.md)). |
| **Public computer** | A community computer anyone may use, such as one in the Lodge. It has a guest account that wipes itself at logout. |
| **Private device** | A phone, laptop, tablet, or other device a resident or visitor owns. The Linux and BSD rule does not apply to it. |
| **Linux** | A free, open-source operating system family, such as Debian, Ubuntu, Fedora, or Linux Mint. |
| **BSD** | A family of free, open-source operating systems: FreeBSD, OpenBSD, and NetBSD. |
| **Sealed appliance** | A device whose built-in software cannot be replaced, such as a payment processor's card reader. Allowed, but kept on its own network and never used for community records. |
