# Federation of Villages

## Rule

**Two or more self-governing villages may form a federation:** a voluntary union with a short charter of shared principles, a council of delegates, and shared services. Each village keeps its own charter, land, money, members, and tiers. The federation never governs the villages. It does only what its charter lists, and anything else stays with each village.

**A federation is lawful, not sovereign.** Every village and the federation itself stay under the laws of the country, state, and county they are in. They pay their taxes, follow permits and court orders, and use legal forms of organization (see [Legal Forms](#legal-forms)).

This page is a bonus. It sits outside the tier triggers. Each village decides to join by its own big-decision rules: a supermajority, a cooling-off period, and only members past the voting waiting period voting ([Chapter 5](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/01-membership-and-conduct.md)). Record the decision in the [revision log](../../REVISION_LOG.md).

## What a Federation Is, and Is Not

| A federation is | A federation is not |
|---|---|
| A voluntary union that any village can leave | A government over the villages |
| A shared charter of principles every member keeps | A nation, or a claim to be one |
| A council of delegates for shared matters | A way around taxes, permits, zoning, or courts |
| Shared services: buying, training, mediation, mutual aid | A pool of the villages' money, land, or members |
| A shared name and identity | A replacement for each village's own charter |

**No sovereignty claims, ever.** No village or federation:

- Claims to be a sovereign nation, or to be outside the host country's laws
- Refuses taxes, permits, inspections, or court orders on that basis
- Issues anything that looks like a passport, driver's license, or other government document

Courts consistently reject sovereignty claims. Making one can cost a village its land, its tax exemption, and its insurance, and invites the "cult" or "compound" label the handbook works to avoid ([Outside Harassment and Community Security](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/06-outside-harassment-and-community-security.md)). Being openly lawful is what protects the villages.

## When to Federate

- [ ] **Founding members** are villages at **Tier 2 or above** whose sister-village agreements, or other written partnerships, have worked well for at least a year ([Sister Villages](sister-villages.md))
- [ ] **Newer villages,** including a sister village still at Tier 0 or Tier 1, join as **associate members:** they take part in the council and the shared services, but don't vote until they reach Tier 2
- [ ] Every village has voted to join by its own big-decision rules
- [ ] An attorney has reviewed the charter of shared principles and the chosen legal form

## The Charter of Shared Principles

Keep it short. Each village's own charter does the detailed work.

**Principles every member village keeps**

- **Equal treatment** of every person, whatever they believe, and no building dedicated to a single belief ([Chapter 3](../part-1-core-chapters/03-charter-and-decision-making.md#equal-treatment-and-shared-space))
- **Safeguarding** that reaches every adult, including leaders, with concerns passed to the right authority and never hidden ([Safeguarding](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/02-safeguarding-children-and-vulnerable-adults.md))
- **Two people for every critical job,** and no one person holding the money, keys, and decisions
- **A fair conduct and removal process,** with an independent appeal
- **Open books:** an annual report each year, shared with members and on request ([Appendix D10](../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d10-annual-report-template.md))
- **Lawful operation,** with no sovereignty claims
- **A free exit:** any member may leave their village, and any village may leave the federation, without penalty beyond settling what is owed

**Village first.** A decision belongs to each village unless the federation charter says otherwise. When in doubt, it stays with the village.

## The Federation Council

- [ ] **Two delegates from each village,** elected by its governance meeting for set terms, so no one person speaks for a village alone. A village can recall its delegates at any time.
- [ ] **One village, one vote** on everyday federation matters
- [ ] **A double majority for big federation decisions,** such as admitting or suspending a village, changing the charter, or setting dues: a supermajority of villages, and villages that together hold a majority of all members. Neither the small villages nor the large ones can push a decision through alone.
- [ ] **Changes to the charter of shared principles** are also ratified by each village's own vote before they take effect
- [ ] **Delegates can't bind their village** to anything beyond the federation charter. Anything new goes back to each village to vote on.
- [ ] **Meets each season,** hosted by each village in turn, with written minutes shared with every member of every village
- [ ] **A chair and a secretary** from different villages, rotating each year

**What the council decides:** the federation budget and dues, the shared services and their rules, applications to join, the mediation and appeals roster, the joint training calendar, and coordination in an emergency. Nothing else.

## Shared Services

Each one is optional, and each has written rules and a named lead from one village with a backup from another:

- **A market cooperative:** a shared brand, stand, or delivery route, with each village's sales and sales tax kept separate ([Chapter 6](../part-2-market-engine/06-market-business-basics/README.md))
- **Bulk buying** of seed, gravel, block, drywall, and equipment
- **An equipment library** with a shared sign-out list and repair fund
- **A labor exchange network** across all the villages, with hours recorded in each village's [volunteer-hours log](../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d07-contribution-and-volunteer-hours-log.md) and balanced over the year ([Sister Villages](sister-villages.md#what-the-villages-share))
- **A training school:** first aid and wilderness first aid, mediation, safeguarding, firearms safety, bookkeeping, and building skills
- **A mediation and appeals roster:** trained people from other villages who hear a village's appeals, so the hearing is always independent
- **Mutual aid:** a joint disaster plan, with shelter, water, food, and transport shared in an emergency ([Appendix D15](../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d15-disaster-plan-template.md))
- **A seed bank** of open-pollinated seed saved across the villages
- **A fiber backbone** linking the villages' networks, with a firewall at each village ([Digital Infrastructure](digital-infrastructure.md#fiber-optic-network))
- **Mentoring new villages,** so every new group starts with experienced help
- **A shared land trust,** if the villages choose one, holding land with a separate long ground lease and board seats for each village

## Money

- **Dues** from each village, by a formula in the charter (for example, per planned lot), capped at a small share of each village's yearly budget
- **Its own account,** with two signers from different villages, its own budget, and an annual report
- **The federation never holds a village's money, reserve, or property tax fund,** and never draws on them
- **No loans between villages** without each village's own board approval, and never from an operating reserve
- **Shared costs split by written formulas,** recorded in every village's books

## Legal Forms

The federation needs a legal home. Ask an attorney which fits:

| Option | How it works | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Association of villages | A written agreement among the village organizations | Members may share liability, so check insurance |
| Nonprofit federation | A nonprofit whose members are the village nonprofits or land trusts | Its own board, filings, and annual report |
| Secondary cooperative | A cooperative owned by the village cooperatives, often for the market and bulk buying | Cooperative law and member votes |
| Shared land trust | Holds land for several villages, with ground leases | Board seats for each village, and long lease terms |
| Special district | A public agency, such as a water or fire district, serving the villages | Public-agency rules, elections, and open meetings |
| Incorporated town or village | Where state law allows and the population meets the minimum, the area becomes a real local government, with powers such as zoning | It must follow election, open-meeting, and public-records laws, and every resident votes, not only members. Its own laws and equal rights for all residents come before any federation principle. |

**Incorporation is the only path to real local government.** It is also the biggest change: the town answers to all its residents, not to the villages' members. Treat it as a separate decision, with its own legal advice.

## Shared Identity

- A **name, flag, and emblem** for the federation, alongside each village's own
- A **founding day** and a yearly **gathering** of every village, to share lessons, celebrate, and review the charter
- **Member cards,** if wanted, as keepsakes. They must say clearly that they are not a government document, and must not look like a passport or ID.
- Symbols, songs, and ceremonies **open to every member on equal terms,** favoring no belief ([Equal Treatment](../part-1-core-chapters/03-charter-and-decision-making.md#equal-treatment-and-shared-space))

## Joining, Leaving, and Suspension

**Joining:** a village applies in writing, shows its charter, latest annual report, and tier, and is admitted by the council's double majority.

**Leaving:** a village may leave with written notice (for example, 12 months), after settling its share of shared costs. Shared equipment and assets are divided by the charter's formula, and any land lease continues on its own terms.

**Suspension:** a village that breaks a core principle, such as covering up a safeguarding concern or making a sovereignty claim, gets written notice, a hearing with an outside mediator, and time to put it right. Only then can the council suspend or remove it, by double majority. Its members' own rights in their village are never affected.

## Disputes Between Villages

1. **Talk directly,** between the two villages' delegates
2. **Council mediation,** by delegates from villages not involved
3. **An outside mediator,** agreed by both villages
4. **Never** in public, online, or through economic pressure, such as cutting a village out of the market or the labor exchange while the dispute is open

## Tier Fit

- **Villages at Tier 0-1:** join as associate members, without a vote, and use the shared services and mentoring
- **Villages at Tier 2-3:** may found a federation or join as full members
- **Villages at Tier 4:** often anchor the federation, mentor new villages, and host the shared land trust
- **Every year:** a gathering of all villages and a review of the charter, the dues, and each shared service

## Common Failures

- The federation turning into a government over the villages, deciding things the charter never gave it
- The largest village always getting its way, or small villages blocking every decision
- Sovereignty talk, or keepsake cards that look like official ID
- Delegates promising things their village never voted on
- Dues that keep growing, or a federation account that starts holding village money
- No way for a village to leave, so a strained relationship has nowhere to go
- Using the market or the labor exchange to punish a village during a dispute
- Incorporating as a town without understanding that every resident, member or not, then has a vote

## Money and Legal Tie-In

- Record dues, shared costs, and shared assets in every village's books and in the federation's annual report
- Ask an accountant how dues and shared services are treated, and whether they affect any village's tax exemption
- Check insurance for shared events, equipment, and the labor exchange

> Rules on associations, nonprofits, cooperatives, land trusts, special districts, and incorporating a town differ widely by country and state. This handbook gives no legal advice, so have an attorney review the charter of shared principles and the legal form before any village joins.
