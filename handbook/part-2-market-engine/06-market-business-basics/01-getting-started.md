# Getting Started

## Principle
**The market is the income engine, so treat it like a small business from the first sale.** Sell a few things well, follow the food rules, keep honest records, and grow only when the numbers support it. Rules differ by place, so confirm local requirements before the first sale (Chapter 4).

## Before the First Sale
- [ ] Consult an accountant about taxes and record-keeping (Chapter 8)
- [ ] Contact the market manager: vendor rules, fees, application deadlines, and waitlists
- [ ] Confirm the vendor permit or license, if required
- [ ] Check food rules: what may be sold raw, what counts as "processed," and any inspection, labeling, or training requirements
- [ ] Register for sales tax if required, and learn what is taxable (some places exempt unprocessed produce)
- [ ] Get liability insurance, since many markets require proof
- [ ] Open the dedicated community account (two signers, Chapter 2)

## What to Sell
| Product | Typical rules | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh produce | Usually the simplest | Best Tier 1 product. Wash, sort, and pack well (Chapter 7). |
| Seedlings and plants | Often simple. Some places require a nursery license or plant-health check. | Strong income. Starts in the hoop house. Count trays so loss is noticed (Chapter 7). |
| Eggs, honey, and similar | Often have specific rules | Check before adding. Eggs need animals, which are optional and Tier 3 or later (Chapter 5). |
| Preserves, baked goods, and other processed food | Cottage food or kitchen rules often apply | Needs a compliant kitchen, labels, and sometimes training. Add at Tier 2. |
| Meat, dairy, and prepared meals | Usually strictly regulated | Not in the early tiers |
| Crafts and member-made art | Usually fewest rules | Run as a craft table beside the produce stand |

Start with two or three products and add more only after those sell steadily.

## Pricing
- Know your cost per item: seed, water, packaging, fees, and labor time
- Check what other vendors charge, and don't undercut them. It hurts the market and your neighbors.
- Price to cover costs plus the reserve, not just to sell out
- Use round numbers and clear signs, and bundle slow sellers ("3 for $5")
- Review prices each season, and keep the sales log to see what actually earns

## Booth Setup
- Table, canopy, **weights or anchors for the canopy (wind lifts them, and canopies come down in high wind, Chapter 16),** and a sign with the community's name
- Clean cloths, food-safe containers, and covered displays
- Hand-wash station and sanitizer, if food is handled
- Scale if selling by weight (confirm it is legal for trade where you are)
- Signs showing price and any required labels
- A box for receipts and the sales log
- A first-aid kit, shade, water, and a chair for the seller
