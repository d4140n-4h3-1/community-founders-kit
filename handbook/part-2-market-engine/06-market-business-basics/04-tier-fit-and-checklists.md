# Tier Fit and Checklists

## Tier Fit
- **Tier 0:** sell small surplus if permitted, after consulting an accountant. Keep the sales log from the first sale.
- **Tier 1:** weekly market table and canopy, with produce and seedlings. Add the craft table. Register for sales tax and permits, and use a dedicated account.
- **Tier 2:** add value-added products from a compliant kitchen or wash-and-pack station. Start regular surplus donations.
- **Tier 3:** add a second market day or a second market, and track which stand earns more per hour.
- **Tier 4:** review product mix and prices yearly, mentor new groups on market basics, and publish results in the annual report.

## Weekly Market-Day Checklist
- [ ] Harvest and pack the day before, and keep it cool
- [ ] Check the wind forecast, and anchor or skip the canopy if it is high
- [ ] Load: table, canopy, weights, signs, scale, cash float, receipts, log, and first-aid kit
- [ ] Two people count the float
- [ ] Set up early, with hand-wash station and sanitizer
- [ ] Record sales as they happen
- [ ] Two people count the cash at close
- [ ] Donate or compost unsold perishables
- [ ] Deposit takings and update the log
- [ ] Debrief: what sold, what didn't, what to change

## Common Failures
- Selling before checking permits, food rules, or sales tax
- Selling too many products too soon
- Pricing below cost, so busy weeks lose money
- One person handling all the cash
- Not recording small sales, so income looks lower than it is
- Selling processed food from an unapproved kitchen
- Wilted produce from poor shade and cooling
- Skipping weeks, so customers stop looking for the stand
- Ignoring complaints from the market manager or customers
- Mixing community money with personal money
- An unweighted canopy that becomes a hazard in a gust

## Money Tie-In
- Market income pays operating costs first, then the reserve (Chapter 8)
- Track the cost per item, so product choices follow the numbers
- Include sales, donations, and tax paid in the annual report

> Market, food, and tax rules differ by location and by product. This handbook gives no legal or tax advice, so confirm what applies with the market manager, the health department, and an accountant before selling.
