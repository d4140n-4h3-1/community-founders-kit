# Tier Fit and Checklists

## Tier Fit
- **Tier 0:** one or two small beds or containers, hand tools, easy crops, low tunnels or cold frames. Feed the community first, and sell only surplus.
- **Tier 1:** a market plot, the hoop house, drip irrigation, and seedlings. Three to five market crops, with a crop plan and harvest log.
- **Tier 2:** a greenhouse, the wash-and-pack station, succession planting, and cold storage for harvest. Start fruit trees and berries, and consider value-added products. Add crop protection basics.
- **Tier 3:** more growing space, a heated greenhouse for year-round work, and a second market day. Track yield and labor per crop.
- **Tier 4:** a multi-year rotation plan, a yearly production review, and mentoring for new groups.

## Seasonal Production Checklist
- **Winter:** review last season's numbers, update the crop plan, order seed, soil test, repair tools, test alarms and vent openers
- **Early spring:** start seedlings, prepare beds, set up irrigation, and check frost dates
- **Spring and early summer:** plant in batches, mulch, begin weekly pest checks, and start market sales
- **Summer:** water on schedule, harvest often, sow fall crops, and protect crops and workers from heat
- **Fall:** harvest storage crops, cure and store them, sow cover crops, and plant garlic or trees where suited
- **Year-end:** log yields and costs, clean and store tools, and hold the production review

## Common Failures
- Planting more than members can tend
- Choosing crops by what is fun rather than what sells
- No soil test, then discovering poor soil or contamination too late
- No irrigation plan, so one dry week ruins the crop
- Raw manure on food beds, or compost too close to wells
- Spraying broadly and killing the spiders and beneficials that were helping
- Selling seedlings too early or too late for the planting season
- Weak cooling after harvest, so produce wilts before market
- No harvest log, so a complaint can't be traced
- One member knowing the whole plan, then leaving
- Using the word "organic" without checking the local rules
- Growing food on the septic field or near old fuel or dump sites

## Money Tie-In
- Track seed, water, tools, packaging, alarms, and labor hours in the expense log
- Match crop choices to the sales log (Chapter 6), so the best earners get the best beds
- Include harvest totals, food donated, and the community's own food share in the annual report (Chapter 8)

> Growing conditions, food-safety rules, and pesticide laws differ by location. Confirm what applies with the agricultural extension service, the health department, and the market manager before planting for sale.
