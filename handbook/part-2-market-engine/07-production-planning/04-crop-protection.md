# Crop Protection

**Principle:** **Security and crop protection are separate jobs.** Locks, lights, and cameras (Chapters [5](../../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md) and [9](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/09-shelter-evolution/README.md)) stop casual damage and theft. They do almost nothing against the usual causes of crop loss: overheating, frost, power or water failure, disease, pests, wind, and human error. Plan for both.

**What actually causes most greenhouse crop loss**
- A stuck or unvented greenhouse overheating, or a failed heater letting frost in
- Power or water failure (pumps, timers, fans, vent openers)
- Disease and pests spreading in warm, humid air
- Human error, such as a door left open or overwatering
- Wind tearing covers and breaking panels

**Rules**
- [ ] **Temperature alarm:** a cheap sensor that sounds or sends a text when the greenhouse gets too hot or too cold. It also catches tampering with vents or heaters.
- [ ] **Backup for power and water:** battery or solar vent openers, a stored water reserve, and a manual way to open vents
- [ ] **Twice-daily check:** one named person checks vents, doors, temperature, water, and plants each morning and evening, logged on the chore chart ([Appendix A11](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a11-weekly-chore-chart.md)) and the crop protection checklist ([Appendix A18](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a18-greenhouse-security-and-crop-protection.md))
- [ ] **Protect the supply:** lock the pump house, shutoffs, and breaker boxes, since they are easier targets than the greenhouse itself ([Chapter 10](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/10-water-plumbing-and-septic/README.md))
- [ ] **Counts and photos:** a tray count and dated photos, so loss or damage is spotted early and can be shown to an insurer
- [ ] **Clean entry routine:** a footbath or boot change at the greenhouse door, and clean tools, so people don't carry pests in
- [ ] **Anchor and brace** covers and panels against the prevailing wind, with windbreaks placed safely ([Chapter 16](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/16-wind-power-and-wind-safety.md))
- [ ] **Never leave heaters or lights running unattended** unless a thermostat and the fire rules protect them ([Chapter 9](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/09-shelter-evolution/README.md))
- [ ] **Insurance:** ask whether crops and greenhouse structures can be covered, and what security the insurer expects ([Chapter 4](../../part-1-core-chapters/04-legal-basics.md))

**If crops are damaged on purpose**
- Treat it as an incident: log it with photos and footage in the outside incident log ([Appendix D16](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d16-outside-incident-and-harassment-log.md)), and tell the spokesperson and the safeguarding lead if a resident may be involved
- Report threats or repeated vandalism to the authorities
- Don't confront anyone alone ([Chapter 5](../../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md))
- Check water, soil, and plants for tampering before anything is sold or eaten, and when in doubt, don't sell it

**Tier fit**
- **Tier 1:** a thermometer and the twice-daily check in the hoop house, with valuables and stock removed overnight
- **Tier 2:** a temperature alarm, a battery vent opener, a stored water reserve, and the clean entry routine in the seedling greenhouse
- **Tier 3:** alarms and backups in every greenhouse, tray counts in the sales log, and insurance for crops and structures reviewed
- **Tier 4:** review losses and causes yearly in the production review

**Common failures**
- Relying on a lock and camera to protect crops from heat and frost
- No temperature alarm, so a stuck vent cooks a whole house of seedlings
- Pumps and breakers left unlocked while the greenhouse is secured
- One person who knows how to open the vents or restart the water
- No counts or photos, so a loss can't be proved
- Tools and chemicals stored in the greenhouse
- Selling plants or produce after a suspected tampering without checking
