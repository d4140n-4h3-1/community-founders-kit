# Site and Crop Choice

## Principle
**Grow what the community can eat and the market will buy, in amounts the members can actually tend.** Start small, choose forgiving crops, keep records, and add acreage only when the numbers and the people support it. A small bed that is weeded, watered, and harvested on time beats a large one that is not.

## Know the Site Before Planting
- [ ] **Soil test** from a lab: nutrients, pH, and any contamination (old land can hold lead, fuel, or other residues, so match this to the environmental check in Chapter 13)
- [ ] **Sun:** note hours of direct light in each bed area across the seasons
- [ ] **Water:** a reliable source near the beds (Chapter 10)
- [ ] **Climate dates:** last spring frost and first fall frost, or the start and end of the rainy and dry seasons
- [ ] **Drainage and slope:** avoid low spots that flood and hilltops that dry out
- [ ] **Wind and animals:** wind breaks, fencing, and deer or rabbit pressure (Chapter 16)
- [ ] **Keep food crops off the septic drain field,** and away from fuel storage and old dumps

## Choosing Crops
Pick crops that grow easily where you are, sell at your market, pay back quickly, and forgive mistakes.

| Crop type | Why it fits | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Salad greens and herbs | Fast, repeat harvests, high return per square foot | Need quick cooling and gentle handling |
| Radishes, beans, summer squash, cucumbers | Quick, productive, easy to start | Heavy picking schedule in peak weeks |
| Tomatoes and peppers | Popular and well priced | Need support, watering, and pest watching |
| Potatoes, onions, garlic, winter squash | Store well and suit the food store | Slower, and need curing and dry storage |
| Seedlings, herbs, and flowers | Strong early-season income from the hoop house | Timing is everything (see Seedlings below) |
| Fruit trees and berries | Long-term income and food | Take years to pay back, so plant early and expect a wait |

Ask the market manager, regular customers, and the local agricultural extension service which varieties do well nearby.

## Plan Backward From the Market
1. Ask the manager and customers what sells out or is missing at the market.
2. Look at what other vendors grow, and choose crops that complement theirs.
3. Pick **three to five crops** for the first season.
4. Estimate how many weeks each crop can be harvested, and how much you can realistically sell per market day.
5. Work back from the market date to sowing and planting dates (Appendix C).
6. Set the **community share** first: the portion of each crop reserved for the community's own meals and the food store. Plan the market share from what remains.
