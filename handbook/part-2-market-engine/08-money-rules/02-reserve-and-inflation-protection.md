# Reserve and Inflation Protection

**Principle:** **Liquidity first, protection second, speculation never.** The reserve exists to carry the community through a bad season. Inflation protection matters, but only after the community can pay its bills without selling anything. The best inflation hedge is usually lower costs: assets that cut expenses every year.

**Reserve layers (in order of priority)**

| Layer | Purpose | Where it lives | Can it be converted? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Operating reserve | Covers 2-6 months of costs (see tier triggers) | Cash or insured accounts | **Never** |
| 2. Cost-cutting investments | Lower yearly expenses | Solar, insulation, LEDs, wash-and-pack station, water systems | Not for sale |
| 3. Inflation reserve | Surplus beyond the 6-month Tier 4 reserve | Inflation-linked or short-term government bonds, where available | Yes, by board vote |
| 4. Small store-of-value holdings | Long-term hedge, capped share of the surplus | Precious metals, per the rules below | Only by board vote |

**Rules for all holdings**

- [ ] Layers 3 and 4 open only after the Tier 3 → 4 reserve (6 months of costs) is full and Layer 2 needs are met
- [ ] Set a cap in the charter on the share of surplus that may go into Layer 4, and review it yearly
- [ ] **Every purchase and sale needs a board vote that includes non-residents**, with the reasons recorded in the revision log
- [ ] Two-person custody for any physical asset: no single member holds access
- [ ] Written inventory, signed at least yearly, with photos and serial numbers where they exist ([Appendix D8](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d08-asset-inventory-and-transaction-log.md))
- [ ] Record every transaction (date, dealer or seller, amount, price, and storage location)
- [ ] Insure physical assets, and store them off-site (such as a bank safe-deposit box) or in a locked, secured space
- [ ] Never borrow to buy a store-of-value asset
- [ ] No member may buy from or sell to the community personally (conflict of interest)

**Precious metals (Layer 4)**

- Gold and silver only, in standard bars or government-minted coins that dealers recognize
- Buy from reputable, established dealers, and compare prices across several
- Expect a spread of a few percent each way, so treat it as a multi-year hold
- Prices can fall for years, so metals never cover bad-season costs
- Check how your location taxes purchases and sales (some places add sales tax or treat metals as collectibles)
- **Stones are excluded.** They are hard to appraise, have few buyers, and usually resell far below purchase price.

**Art**

- **Art is never part of the operating or Tier 4 reserve.**
- **Member-made work:** sell it at the market. A craft table beside the produce stand turns skills into income.
- **Commissioned pieces:** pay local artists for murals, signs, and decor that the community uses now. Any later gain is a bonus.
- **Donated pieces:** accept them, record them, and get a written appraisal before counting them as assets.
- **Purchases for investment:** discouraged. If proposed, they need a board vote, an independent appraisal, and a provenance record (documented ownership history).
- Appraise and insure any piece above a value set in the charter, and keep a signed inventory.
- Display or store valuable pieces securely. The food store's door, roof, and key-log rules apply here too.
- Expect high selling costs (often 10-30% or more at auction or through dealers) and slow sales.

**Legal and tax checks**

- Ask an attorney and an accountant whether your nonprofit or land trust rules limit non-income-producing assets or require prudent-investor standards
- Check how donated art and metals are reported and taxed locally
- Keep the reserve policy in the community files, and update it when the law or the budget changes

**Tier fit**

- **Tier 0-2:** no store-of-value purchases. Build the cash reserve in an insured account, reaching 2 months of costs before Tier 3, and build the cost-cutting systems.
- **Tier 3:** write the reserve policy into the charter, and grow the reserve toward 6 months of costs.
- **Tier 4:** if the 6-month reserve is full, the board may consider Layer 3, then a capped Layer 4. Review yearly.
- **Graduation:** record what worked and what didn't, and add it to the handbook.

**Common failures**

- Converting the operating reserve into metals, then needing cash during a price drop
- Buying stones or art with no independent appraisal
- One member holding the only key to the stored assets
- No inventory, so no one knows what the community owns
- Buying at a retail markup and selling in a panic at a discount
- Skipping the attorney review and breaking nonprofit investment rules
- Treating a hedge as income, and spending it like income

> Prices of metals, stones, and art can fall as well as rise. This handbook gives no financial advice. Have a qualified accountant, attorney, or financial advisor review the policy before the community acts on it.
