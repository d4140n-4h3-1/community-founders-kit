# Community Fee and Property Tax

**Rule:** **If the community owns land and has no property tax exemption, residents pay a monthly community fee that covers the property tax bill first.** The fee is set per planned lot (one household or cabin is one lot, not one person) from the real bill, written down, and applied the same way to everyone. **Only residents pay it.** Outside supporters may donate, but donations are never counted on for the tax bill.

**First ask whether the fee is needed at all**

- Many nonprofits and land trusts charge residents lot fees or ground-lease fees and **keep** their exemption. If the exemption holds, the fee may not be needed for property tax.
- Do not leave nonprofit status just to charge a fee. Have an accountant compare both paths first, including what leaving would cost in lost exemptions, grants, and board structure.
- Start the fee only if the exemption is **denied or lapses**.

**How to set the fee** (worksheet in [Appendix D11](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d11-fee-calculation-worksheet.md))

- Start with the annual property tax bill
- Add a **buffer of 15-20%**, because bills rise, plus a **vacancy allowance**, because some lots will sit empty
- Add the share of insurance and the reserve contribution, if the board chooses
- **Divide by planned lots, not current residents.** If the fee is split among whoever is currently paying, every departure raises everyone else's share, and that can start a spiral where more people leave. Cover vacancies from the buffer instead.
- Then divide by 12
- Example: a $6,000 tax bill + 20% buffer = $7,200. Across 20 planned lots, that is $360 a year, or **$30 a month per lot**.
- Recalculate every year when the new bill arrives, and announce changes well in advance

**Property tax fund** (ledger in [Appendix D12](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d12-property-tax-fund-ledger.md))

- [ ] Fees go into a **dedicated property tax fund**, never into general spending
- [ ] **Before the first fee is due, the fund holds at least one full year's tax bill.** The first bill should never depend on this month's collections.
- [ ] Pay the tax bill from the fund, and keep the receipt in the files
- [ ] Add the bill's due date to the compliance calendar, with two responsible members (a missed deadline brings penalties and liens)
- [ ] Publish the fee calculation each year, so residents see exactly where the money goes

**Legal checks (before the first fee)**

- [ ] Does the fee count as rent or lot rent under local law, and what tenant rights and eviction rules follow?
- [ ] Do zoning or licensing rules for a housing, RV, or manufactured-home community apply?
- [ ] Is the fee taxable income, and does any lodging or sales tax apply?
- [ ] Would a change from a nonprofit to another structure affect the land, the board, or any grants? Does the exemption end, and when?
- [ ] Fair-housing and anti-discrimination rules: the same fee and terms for everyone
- [ ] Have an attorney draft the residents' agreement ([Appendix B5](../../appendices/appendix-b-templates/b05-residents-agreement.md))

**Structure options (ask an attorney which fits)**

| Option | How the fee works | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Nonprofit or land trust with lot fees | Residents pay a lot or ground-lease fee, and the exemption may stay | Confirm the fee doesn't break the exemption rules |
| Cooperative | Members own a share and pay monthly charges | Needs clear bylaws and a vote on fees |
| Land trust with ground leases | Residents lease their spot and pay a lease fee | Long-term lease terms, resale rules |
| LLC or association | Members pay dues under an operating agreement | Liability and tax treatment |

**Rules**

- [ ] A written agreement that states the fee, what it covers, and how it changes
- [ ] Missed payments: a reminder, a payment plan, and a board review before any removal action, following the fair process in [Chapter 5](../../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md). Quick, fair handling prevents the resentment that breaks communities.
- [ ] **Hardship option:** a reduced fee or work-trade, set by written rules and applied the same way to everyone
- [ ] No fee may be changed in secret or for one person
- [ ] Fees are not the only income. Market income and the reserve carry the bad months, so one weak season doesn't threaten the land.
- [ ] Fees never fund store-of-value purchases (the reserve rules above still apply)

**Tier fit**

- **Tier 0-2:** no fees tied to property tax, since the community is leasing or exempt. Voluntary contributions only, and the accountant confirms how they are treated.
- **Tier 3:** write the fee policy into the charter, compare the nonprofit and non-exempt paths with an accountant, and get the attorney review done before buying land.
- **Tier 4:** build the one-year tax fund first. Start the fee only if the exemption is denied or lapses. Review yearly.

**Common failures**

- Dividing the bill by current residents, so every departure raises everyone's share
- Setting the fee below the real tax bill, or forgetting that taxes rise
- Starting the fee with no first-year tax fund, so one late payment becomes a crisis
- Leaving nonprofit status without comparing the costs
- Calling it a "donation" when residents are required to pay it
- Using the fund for other costs, then missing a tax deadline
- Applying hardship rules unevenly, which causes resentment and legal risk
- No agreement, so no one knows what a missed payment means
- Counting on outside donations to cover the bill

> Fee rules, tenant protections, and tax treatment vary widely by location. This handbook gives no legal or tax advice, so have an attorney and an accountant confirm the structure before any fee is collected.
