# Chickens

*Chickens for eggs and pest control. Optional, under the [animal rules](09-animals.md).*

A small flock of hens gives the community eggs and eats insects, grubs, slugs, and weed seeds. Chickens are kept under the [animal rules](09-animals.md): Tier 3 or later, a group vote, a care lead and a backup, a daily rota, and a budget.

## Eggs

- **No rooster is needed** for hens to lay. Roosters are loud, and many places ban them. If a rooster is kept, he lives apart from the hens until the group decides to hatch chicks (see [Breeding](09-animals.md#breeding)).
- **Expect fewer eggs** in winter, while hens molt, and as they age. A hen lays well for a few years but can live for many more, so decide before starting what happens to hens that stop laying.
- **Collect eggs every day,** keep nests clean, throw out cracked or dirty eggs, and refrigerate eggs that are kept
- **Eggs are a regulated food.** The community's own share is fine, but eggs are sold or given outside the households only under the local egg rules, which often cover licensing, labeling, cleaning, and cold storage ([Chapter 6](../../part-2-market-engine/06-market-business-basics/01-getting-started.md))

## Pest Control, Without the Damage

Chickens scratch up seedlings, eat young plants and ripe fruit, and dust-bathe in soft beds. Put them to work where that doesn't matter:

- [ ] **In a garden bed before planting or after harvest,** to clear insects, grubs, and weed seeds, then out again
- [ ] **Under established fruit trees,** where they eat fallen fruit and the pests in it
- [ ] **In a movable coop or a pen of electric netting** (a chicken tractor), moved every few days so no patch is scratched bare
- [ ] **Out of the growing beds in season,** and away from the spider habitat the handbook protects, since chickens eat spiders too ([Chapter 15](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/15-porch-lights-and-garden-spiders.md))

## Keeping Chickens

- [ ] **Never just one.** Chickens are flock animals. Start small, for example with four to six hens.
- [ ] **A predator-proof coop:** hardware cloth (welded wire mesh), not chicken wire, which keeps hens in but not raccoons, foxes, or weasels out. A buried or turned-out apron of mesh, a locked door at dusk (an automatic door helps), and a covered run where hawks hunt.
- [ ] **Room to live:** roughly 4 square feet (0.4 square meters) per bird inside the coop and 10 square feet (1 square meter) per bird in the run, perches, about one nest box for every four hens, and ventilation without drafts
- [ ] **Where the coop goes:** away from drinking water, the wash-and-pack station, the food store, and the septic field, and downwind of homes
- [ ] **Food and water:** layer feed, crushed oyster shell for strong eggshells, grit, and clean water every day, thawed in freezing weather. Feed is stored in sealed metal bins, since it draws rodents.
- [ ] **Daily rota:** let the hens out, feed and water them, collect the eggs, and lock the coop at dusk. These are essential tasks, done every day, rest days included.
- [ ] **Hygiene:** everyone washes their hands after handling chickens, eggs, or the coop, since poultry can carry salmonella. Children under 5 don't handle chickens, and no poultry comes into homes or kitchens.
- [ ] **Bird flu:** keep wild birds away from the feed and water, use separate boots for the coop, and report sick or dead birds to the vet or the agriculture authority
- [ ] **Health:** a vet or extension adviser who knows poultry, and a check of the flock each day for injury or illness
- [ ] **Manure** composted before it goes near food crops, since fresh chicken manure is strong enough to burn plants and can carry germs ([Chapter 7](../../part-2-market-engine/07-production-planning/README.md))
- [ ] **Predators:** prevention first, under the predator plan ([Firearms Safety](05-firearms-safety.md))
- [ ] **Check local rules** on flock size, roosters, coop setbacks, and selling eggs
