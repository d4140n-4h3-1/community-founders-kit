# Guinea Fowl

*Guinea fowl for pest control and early warning. Optional, under the [animal rules](09-animals.md).*

Guinea fowl are hardy birds that roam in a flock and eat ticks, grasshoppers, beetles, and other insects. They scratch far less than chickens, so they do little harm to established plants. They are also loud: a guinea flock raises an alarm at any stranger, predator, or snake, which makes them a noisy early warning. Guinea fowl are kept under the [animal rules](09-animals.md): Tier 3 or later, a group vote, a care lead and a backup, a daily rota, and a budget.

## Pest Control

- [ ] **Let them range** over pasture, orchards, field edges, and around established plantings, where they hunt ticks and insects
- [ ] **Keep them off new seedlings and ripe soft fruit,** which they may peck
- [ ] **They eat spiders too.** Keep the protected spider habitat near homes and porches out of their range, as for chickens ([Chapter 15](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/15-porch-lights-and-garden-spiders.md))

## Noise and Neighbors

- [ ] **Talk to neighbors before getting guinea fowl.** Their calls are loud, frequent, and carry far, and they may set off at passers-by, cars, and dogs.
- [ ] **Check local rules** on keeping poultry and on noise, since guinea fowl are louder than most
- [ ] **Keep them from wandering onto neighbors' land,** by training them to come home (below) and keeping them away from boundary fences they can fly over

## Training Them to Come Home

Guinea fowl roam widely and fly well. They learn where home is only if they start there.

- [ ] **Raise them from keets** (chicks), and keep young birds shut in their coop and a covered run for about six weeks before letting them range
- [ ] **Let a few out at first,** and keep the rest in, so the free ones come back to the flock
- [ ] **Feed them in the coop each evening,** so they come back to roost at dusk
- [ ] **Lock them in every night.** Guinea fowl that roost in trees or nest in hidden places are easy prey for owls, foxes, raccoons, and other predators.

## Keeping Guinea Fowl

- [ ] **Never just one.** Guinea fowl are flock birds. Start with a small flock, for example six or more keets, since some may not survive the first weeks.
- [ ] **Keets need care:** a warm, dry brooder, shallow water dishes with pebbles in them so they can't drown, and feed made for game birds or poultry chicks
- [ ] **A predator-proof coop** of hardware cloth, with high perches, since guinea fowl like to roost up high, and room for the whole flock at night
- [ ] **Shared housing with chickens** is possible if both have room, but watch for guinea fowl bullying the chickens
- [ ] **Separate the males,** under the [breeding rule](09-animals.md#breeding). Males and females look alike, but hens make a two-note call that males don't, often from about 8 weeks old, and males usually have larger wattles. Ask an experienced keeper to help tell them apart.
- [ ] **Eggs:** guinea hens lay mostly in spring and summer, often in hidden nests outdoors. The eggs are smaller than hens' eggs and are eaten the same way, under the same egg rules as chickens ([Chickens](13-chickens.md#eggs)).
- [ ] **Hygiene and bird flu,** as for chickens: hands washed after handling birds or eggs, no handling by children under 5, wild birds kept from the feed and water, separate boots for the coop, and sick or dead birds reported ([Chickens](13-chickens.md#keeping-chickens))
- [ ] **Manure** composted before it goes near food crops ([Chapter 7](../../part-2-market-engine/07-production-planning/README.md))

## Common Failures

- Letting guinea fowl out before they know where home is, so they never come back
- Guinea fowl roosting in trees, and lost to owls one by one
- Neighbors who weren't told about the noise
- Keets drowned in a deep water dish
