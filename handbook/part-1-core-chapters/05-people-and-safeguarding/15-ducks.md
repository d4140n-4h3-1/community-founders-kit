# Ducks

*Ducks for eggs and slug control. Optional, under the [animal rules](09-animals.md).*

Ducks are some of the best slug and snail hunters a garden can have, and they also eat insects, mosquito larvae, and weed seeds. Many breeds lay well, often through more of the year than chickens. They are quieter than guinea fowl and calmer than chickens, but they need water and they make a mess. Ducks are kept under the [animal rules](09-animals.md): Tier 3 or later, a group vote, a care lead and a backup, a daily rota, and a budget.

## Pest Control, Without the Damage

Ducks trample soft beds and eat tender greens such as lettuce. Put them to work where that doesn't matter:

- [ ] **In beds before planting, after harvest, or around tall, established crops,** where they clear slugs and snails
- [ ] **Under fruit trees and along paths and field edges**
- [ ] **In a pen of electric netting,** moved every few days, so no patch turns to mud
- [ ] **Out of seedling beds and leafy greens** in season

## Water

- [ ] **Water deep enough to dip their whole head,** every day. Ducks need it to clean their eyes and nostrils. A pond isn't required: a tub or a trough works.
- [ ] **Changed daily.** Ducks muddy their water fast, and dirty water spreads disease.
- [ ] **Kept well away from the drinking water,** the well, the wash-and-pack station, and the food store ([Protecting the Water Supply](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/10-water-plumbing-and-septic/05-safety-maintenance-and-failures.md#protecting-the-water-supply)), and away from the bees' water source ([Bees](17-bees.md))
- [ ] **Dumped where it drains away from buildings and crops,** never onto the septic drain field
- [ ] **Ducklings swim only with someone watching** until they have their adult feathers, since they chill and can drown

## Keeping Ducks

- [ ] **Never just one.** Ducks are flock birds. Start with at least three.
- [ ] **A predator-proof shelter** of hardware cloth, locked every night. Ducks don't perch, so they sleep on the ground on deep, dry bedding, with good airflow, since their breath and water make shelters damp.
- [ ] **A separate shelter from the chickens** where possible, since ducks keep a coop wet
- [ ] **Feed made for ducks or waterfowl.** Ducklings need extra niacin, which chick feed often lacks, so ask the feed supplier or vet.
- [ ] **Separate the drakes (males),** under the [breeding rule](09-animals.md#breeding). Drakes can be rough with the ducks. In most breeds, adult drakes grow a curled tail feather and have a quieter, raspier voice than the ducks.
- [ ] **Eggs:** collect them daily, since ducks often lay early in the morning and anywhere in their bedding. Duck eggs follow the same egg rules as chickens' ([Chickens](13-chickens.md#eggs)).
- [ ] **Bird flu:** ducks and other waterfowl spread bird flu more than most birds. Keep them away from wild ducks and geese and their ponds, use separate boots, and report sick or dead birds to the vet or the agriculture authority.
- [ ] **Hygiene,** as for chickens: hands washed after handling ducks, eggs, or their water, and no handling by children under 5 ([Chickens](13-chickens.md#keeping-chickens))
- [ ] **Manure and dirty bedding** composted before they go near food crops ([Chapter 7](../../part-2-market-engine/07-production-planning/README.md))
- [ ] **Check local rules** on keeping waterfowl, ponds, and selling eggs

## Common Failures

- Water too shallow for ducks to clean their eyes, or left dirty for days
- Duck water dumped near the well or onto the septic field
- A wet, smelly shelter with no fresh bedding or airflow
- Ducks loose in the lettuce bed
- Ducks mixing with wild waterfowl on an open pond during a bird-flu outbreak
