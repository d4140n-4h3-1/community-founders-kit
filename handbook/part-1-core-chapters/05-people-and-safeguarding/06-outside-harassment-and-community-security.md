# Outside Harassment and Community Security

**Principle:** **A small, visible community is easy to bother, so plan calmly before it happens.** Most outside harassment is cheap and low-level: complaints, rumors, trespass, and petty damage. The best defense is to be boringly compliant, well documented, and well liked, and to answer pressure with records, not anger. Harassment between residents is covered by the conduct rules and the conflict ladder above, and it usually matters more than outside threats.

**What to expect**

- **Complaint-based harassment:** anyone can file zoning, building, septic, health, fire, or tax complaints, and each can trigger an inspection even if baseless
- **Online campaigns:** bad reviews, rumors, and labels such as "freeloader" or "cult"
- **Trespass and nuisance:** photographing residents, driving through, dumping trash, or confronting people at the gate or the market stand
- **Theft and vandalism:** unattended gardens, greenhouses, tools, and vehicles
- **Pressure on partners:** calls to the landowner, market manager, or bank asking them to cut ties

**Prevention**

- [ ] **Be compliant:** permits, tested water, inspected septic, filed taxes, and current insurance, so complaints end in clean inspections (Chapters [4](../04-legal-basics.md), [8](../../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md), [10](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/10-water-plumbing-and-septic/README.md))
- [ ] **Be open:** the annual report, open day, and a named neighbor liaison turn hostile neighbors into informed ones ([Chapter 8](../../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md))
- [ ] **Mark boundaries:** posted property lines, black-and-yellow "private property" and "no trespassing" signs at entrances, and a gate policy (check local sign rules)
- [ ] **Sign in visitors,** and keep the visitor welcome sheet at the gate ([Appendix A12](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a12-visitor-welcome-sheet.md))
- [ ] **Secure the valuable and vulnerable places:** the food store, greenhouses, pump house, shutoffs, breaker boxes, and tool shed, with key logs and motion lights ([Chapter 9](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/09-shelter-evolution/README.md))
- [ ] **Audit every key and code once a year:** count every key against the key logs (food store, greenhouses, pump house, gates, fence gates, safes, the network cabinet, and the farm-gun cabinet), take back any that aren't accounted for, and change the codes. Rekey or swap lock cylinders at once when a key is missing or a holder leaves, and on a set schedule for the food store, pump house, and gates. Give emergency services any new lockbox or gate code the same day.
- [ ] **Lock the water and power:** pump houses, shutoffs, and electrical panels are easier targets than the crops they feed
- [ ] **Light and visibility:** motion-sensor lights and clear sight lines from cabins and paths ([Chapter 14](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/14-lighting-standard.md))
- [ ] **Don't depend on one partner:** a second sales outlet and a backup landowner or host by Tier 2 (Chapters [6](../../part-2-market-engine/06-market-business-basics/04-tier-fit-and-checklists.md) and [13](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/13-land-acquisition-and-expansion.md))
- [ ] **Know your allies:** introduce the community to the local police non-emergency contact, the landowner, and the market manager before trouble starts
- [ ] **A trusted attorney** who already knows the community
- [ ] **Privacy:** never publish residents' names, children's photos, or schedules

**Optional fence for growing and storage areas**

A community may fence its growing and storage areas: the gardens and greenhouses, the food store, the pump house, and the tool shed. The rest of the land stays open.

- [ ] **Fence the work areas, not the whole property.** Never fence in homes, block a road, or cross either way out ([Chapter 12](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/12-roads-and-access.md#two-ways-out)).
- [ ] **Gates lock from outside, but always open from inside without a key,** like the [greenhouse rule](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/09-shelter-evolution/09-greenhouse-security.md), so no one is ever locked in
- [ ] **A vehicle gate** wide enough for a fire truck where the fire department asks, with the same lockbox or code as the main gate
- [ ] **The right fence for the job:** welded wire or chain link for security, and about 8 feet (2.4 meters) high where deer are a problem. Bury or turn out the bottom edge where animals dig.
- [ ] **A black-and-yellow sign at each gate.** The motion-light, camera, and key-log rules still apply.
- [ ] **Check local rules** on fence height, setbacks, and materials, and any rules protecting wildlife movement

**Barbed wire on the fences that guard key resources**

The fences around the community's most important resources may carry barbed wire along the top, to stop people climbing over:

- **Where it goes:** the fence around the food store, the pump house and the water and power shutoffs, and the locked store for tools, fuel, and equipment. Along the top of the growing-area fence only where theft has been a real problem.
- **Never:** around homes, the Lodge, the Meeting Hall, children's areas, or paths, on or over any gate, or anywhere along either way out ([Chapter 12](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/12-roads-and-access.md#two-ways-out))

- [ ] **On top only,** at least about 6 feet (1.8 meters) above the ground, so no one brushes against it, and never at child height
- [ ] **Usually three strands on arms angled outward,** away from the protected area, so climbers meet it first and people working inside don't
- [ ] **No razor wire.** It causes far worse injuries, and many places restrict it more than barbed wire.
- [ ] **A black-and-yellow warning sign** reading "BARBED WIRE" on each side of the fence where people might approach it
- [ ] **Check local rules first.** Many places ban or limit barbed wire in residential zones or along public roads, set a minimum height, or require a permit. Ask an attorney and the insurer about liability ([Chapter 4](../04-legal-basics.md)).
- [ ] **Install it safely:** heavy gloves, eye protection, and long sleeves, with two people for every run of wire. Workers keep their tetanus shots up to date.
- [ ] **Walk the fence monthly,** with the food-store check: tighten sagging wire, repair breaks, and free or report any trapped wildlife ([Appendix A8](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a08-food-store-security-and-pests.md))
- [ ] **Keep livestock fencing separate.** Barbed wire can badly injure some animals, such as horses, so pens follow the animal-care rules, not this one ([Daily Life](08-daily-life-chores-shared-spaces-and-routines.md))

**Response plan (write it down)**

1. **One spokesperson and a deputy** speak to officials, press, and neighbors. Everyone else refers questions to them.
2. **Stay calm and do not retaliate.** Never confront, threaten, or follow anyone, and never post angry replies online.
3. **Document everything:** date, time, what happened, photos or footage, and any witnesses, in the outside incident log ([Appendix D16](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d16-outside-incident-and-harassment-log.md)). Save complaints and inspection results together.
4. **Share facts, not feelings:** the annual report, permits, tax filings, and inspection results answer most accusations.
5. **Cooperate with inspectors:** be polite, bring the records, and keep your own notes of what they said.
6. **Escalate in steps:** a calm, documented talk or letter first, then the local authorities for threats, stalking, repeated trespass, or damage, and an attorney for legal threats.
7. **Tell the partners yourself:** if a landowner, market manager, or bank is contacted, give them the facts before they hear rumors.
8. **Protect people:** check on anyone targeted, never leave them alone with a harasser, and follow the safeguarding steps for children ([Chapter 5](README.md)).
9. **Review afterward:** record what worked in the revision log.

**Tier fit**

- **Tier 0:** boundary signs at the entrance, a visitor sign-in, a named spokesperson, and the outside incident log started
- **Tier 1:** a neighbor liaison, an introduction to the police non-emergency contact, and the response plan written into the charter
- **Tier 2:** locks and lights on the food store, greenhouses, and pump house, a trusted attorney on call, the first cameras under the written policy below, and, if the community chooses, a fence around the growing and storage areas, with barbed wire on top of the fences that guard key resources
- **Tier 3:** more cameras under the same policy, and a yearly review of incidents with the board
- **Tier 4:** audit security, incidents, and responses yearly

**Common failures**

- Arguing online instead of showing records
- Several different people speaking to officials or the press
- No incident log, so a pattern can't be shown
- Confronting a trespasser alone
- Missing permits or filings that give a complainant a real finding
- Publishing residents' names, children's photos, or schedules
- A visible, attractive target left unlocked and unlit
- Keys handed on informally for years, so no one knows who can open what
- A changed gate code that emergency services were never told
- A fence gate that can lock someone in, or blocks a fire truck or a way out
- Barbed wire at child height, on a gate, or along a way out, or put up without checking local rules
