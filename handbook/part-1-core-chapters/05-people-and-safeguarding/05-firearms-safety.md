# Firearms Safety

## Principle

**The law is the floor, not the ceiling.** These rules apply even where state and local law are gun friendly. A state that allows open carry, permitless carry, or unlocked storage does not change what the community expects. Owning a firearm is the owner's choice, and the community does not ask anyone to give one up. In return, every firearm on the land is stored, carried, and handled under one written policy that every member and visitor follows (template in [Appendix B11](../../appendices/appendix-b-templates/b11-firearms-policy-template.md)).

The policy sorts firearms into three groups, because each one carries a different risk:

| Group | Examples | The rule |
|---|---|---|
| Semi-automatic rifles and shotguns | Any long gun that reloads itself with each pull of the trigger | **Stored safely, locked, unloaded, whenever it is not in hand** |
| Pistols | Semi-automatic pistols and revolvers | **Carried concealed, or not carried at all** |
| Manual rifles and shotguns | Bolt-action, lever-action, pump-action, break-action, and single-shot | **Legitimate farm tools for protecting livestock, always handled safely, especially rifles** |

## The Four Rules (for every firearm, every time)

1. **Treat every firearm as if it is loaded.** Check it yourself, every time you pick it up or are handed it.
2. **Never point the muzzle at anything you are not willing to destroy,** including people, animals you mean to protect, buildings, and vehicles.
3. **Keep your finger off the trigger until the sights are on the target** and you have decided to shoot.
4. **Know your target and what is beyond it.** A bullet that misses, or passes through, keeps going.

Post these four rules wherever firearms are stored, and teach them to every member and older child, whether or not they will ever handle a gun.

## Semi-Automatic Rifles and Shotguns: Stored Safely

A semi-automatic long gun fires as fast as the trigger is pulled, so the main risk is access by the wrong person: a child, a visitor, a thief, or someone in crisis.

- [ ] **Locked in a gun safe** (steel, with a combination or key lock), bolted to the floor or wall, whenever it is not being carried, cleaned, or used
- [ ] **Unloaded in storage,** with the chamber empty and any magazine removed
- [ ] **Ammunition stored separately,** in its own locked box or a separate locked section of the safe
- [ ] **Keys and combinations** held by the owner only (or the owner and one named adult in the household), never hidden "somewhere obvious" or left in the lock
- [ ] **The safe stays in the owner's own home,** never in the Lodge, the Meeting Hall, the food store, a greenhouse, or a shared shed
- [ ] **Transported unloaded,** in a locked case, with a cable or trigger lock fitted
- [ ] **Not used for livestock protection or daily farm work.** That is what the manual rifles and shotguns are for.
- [ ] **Not carried around the community** for show, patrol, or "security." Outside threats follow the response plan in Outside Harassment and Community Security, not an armed watch.

## Pistols: Concealed if Carried

Carrying is a personal choice. Members who choose to carry follow these rules, so other residents, children, and visitors are not alarmed or put at risk.

- [ ] **Concealed only.** No open carry anywhere on community land, at the market stand, or at community events, even where the law allows it.
- [ ] **Licensed where the law requires it,** with the permit carried while carrying
- [ ] **Carried on the body in a proper holster** that fully covers the trigger guard. Never loose in a pocket, bag, glove box, or waistband without a holster.
- [ ] **Never set down.** A carried pistol stays on the carrier's body. If it has to come off, it goes straight into a locked safe or lockbox.
- [ ] **Never displayed, drawn, or handled in front of others** except to stop an immediate threat to life
- [ ] **Locked up when not carried,** in a safe or a bolted lockbox in the owner's home, with ammunition stored separately
- [ ] **Not carried where the policy bans firearms** (see Where Firearms Are Never Allowed) or where a host, landowner, or market manager forbids them
- [ ] **Training first:** a recognized handgun safety course and regular practice at a range before anyone carries on community land

## Manual Rifles and Shotguns: Farm Tools for Livestock

Bolt, lever, pump, break-action, and single-shot rifles and shotguns are working tools on a farm. They protect livestock from predators and can end the suffering of a badly injured animal when a vet cannot reach it in time. They are treated with the same seriousness as any other dangerous tool, and with more care than most.

**Who may use them**

- [ ] Only **named, trained adults**, usually the animal-care lead and backup ([Animals](09-animals.md)), plus anyone else the board approves
- [ ] Each one has completed a **hunter education or firearms safety course** and practiced with the specific firearm
- [ ] Each one knows the **local law on killing predators,** which animals are protected, and when a permit is needed

**Storage**

- [ ] When not in use, these firearms are **unloaded and locked** in a safe or a locked steel cabinet, with ammunition stored separately
- [ ] During predator season or lambing and calving, a farm gun may be kept in a **locked cabinet near the animals** (in a locked barn, never a shared hall), so it can be reached quickly by its named users
- [ ] **Never left loaded, propped by a door, hung on a rack in the open, or left in a vehicle or a field**

**Handling in the field**

- [ ] **Load only at the place of use,** and unload before walking back, crossing a fence, climbing, or getting into a vehicle
- [ ] **Shoot only with a safe backstop:** solid earth behind the target. Never shoot toward the sky, a ridge or skyline, water, hard ground, rocks, or anything that can cause a ricochet.
- [ ] **Never shoot toward** cabins, the Lodge, the Meeting Hall, roads, paths, gardens, greenhouses, neighbors' land, or other livestock
- [ ] **Hearing and eye protection** when practicing, and warn people nearby before shooting
- [ ] **Two-person rule after dark:** no one goes out alone at night with a firearm. A second person carries the light and keeps others back.

**Rifles need the most care.** A rifle bullet can travel more than a mile and stay dangerous the whole way. A missed shot at a coyote can reach a house, a road, or a neighbor far out of sight. Before a rifle is ever used on the land:

- [ ] Map **safe shooting directions and zones** for each pasture, with the backstops marked, and keep the map with the farm guns
- [ ] Never shoot a rifle from or across a road, or toward any building, however far away it looks
- [ ] Never shoot at a target on a hilltop or skyline, where there is nothing behind it but open air
- [ ] Prefer a **shotgun** for close predator work near buildings and pens. Shot travels a much shorter distance, though slugs still travel far.

**Prevention comes first**

- Most livestock losses are prevented by **good fencing, secure night pens, lights, and guardian animals ([Guardian Animals](10-guardian-animals.md)),** not by shooting. Use them first.
- Remove what draws predators: carcasses, open feed, and uncovered compost ([Chapter 7](../../part-2-market-engine/07-production-planning/README.md)).
- Ask the local wildlife agency or agricultural extension service about non-lethal options and depredation permits.

**After any shot is fired**

- [ ] Unload, and check that no person or animal was hit by mistake
- [ ] Record the date, time, place, reason, firearm, and shooter in the discharge log ([Appendix D19](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d19-firearm-discharge-log.md))
- [ ] Report to the wildlife agency where the law requires it, especially for any protected species
- [ ] Tell nearby neighbors when they may have heard shots, so a predator shot isn't mistaken for a threat

## Where Firearms Are Never Allowed

*(Defaults to adapt in the charter, within local law.)*

- The Lodge, the Meeting Hall, the shared kitchen, the food store, the bathhouse, greenhouses, and children's areas
- Governance meetings, conflict-ladder and removal hearings, and any heated dispute
- The market stand, unless the market's own rules allow it, and then only concealed pistols
- In the hands of anyone affected by alcohol, drugs, or medication that impairs judgment
- In the hands of anyone the law prohibits from having one
- In tents, unless locked in a case that is cabled to something solid

## Children

- [ ] **No child has access** to any firearm, ammunition, key, or combination. Many places have child-access prevention laws that make the adult liable.
- [ ] Teach every child the same simple rule: **"Don't touch. Leave the area. Tell an adult."**
- [ ] Older children learn to shoot only on a youth safety course or with a trained parent, under direct supervision, as the law allows
- [ ] Ask about firearms storage before a child visits another home in the community, and expect the same question in return

## Visitors

- [ ] Visitors follow the same rules. Hosts tell them before they arrive (visitor welcome sheet, [Appendix A12](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a12-visitor-welcome-sheet.md)).
- [ ] A visitor's long gun stays locked in their vehicle or is stored in the host's safe. A visitor's pistol is carried concealed or locked away.
- [ ] No visitor uses a farm gun or shoots on the land

## Safety in a Crisis

- **Suicide is the largest share of firearm deaths in many places.** Locked storage buys time, and time saves lives.
- A member who is struggling, or whose household is in crisis, may ask to have their firearms **stored temporarily off site** with a trusted person, a dealer, or a law enforcement agency, where local law allows. Asking is encouraged and is never held against anyone.
- If someone may be a danger to themselves or others, follow the crisis steps in Health, Safety, and Emergencies and call the crisis line or emergency services. Some places have legal processes for temporarily removing firearms.
- **Brandishing, threatening with, or reaching for a firearm during a dispute** breaks the conduct rules and falls under the immediate safety exception ([Chapter 5, Conflict and Removal](03-conflict-and-removal.md)).

## Records and Upkeep

- Each owner signs a yearly statement that their firearms are stored to this policy ([Appendix B11](../../appendices/appendix-b-templates/b11-firearms-policy-template.md)). The community does **not** keep a list of personal firearms or serial numbers.
- **Community-owned farm guns** are listed, with two named custodians for the cabinet keys, and inspected monthly ([Appendix A21](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a21-firearms-storage-and-farm-gun-inspection.md))
- Clean firearms only after unloading and checking them, in a private space, away from food and children
- **Sales and transfers** between members follow the law for private transfers, including any background-check requirement
- **Tell the insurer** that firearms are kept on the land, and check what coverage and storage it requires ([Chapter 4](../04-legal-basics.md))

## Tier Fit

- **Tier 0:** write the firearms policy into the charter before anyone brings a firearm onto the land. In tents and trailers, firearms stay in a locked case cabled to something solid, in a locked vehicle safe, or off site.
- **Tier 1:** every firearm owner on site has a safe or lockbox. The four rules are posted, and the policy is in the visitor welcome sheet.
- **Tier 2:** cabins have bolted-down safes. Anyone who carries has completed a handgun safety course.
- **Tier 3:** if the group votes to keep livestock ([Animals](09-animals.md)), the predator plan is written: prevention first, named and trained users, a locked cabinet near the animals, the shooting-zone map, and the discharge log.
- **Tier 4:** review the policy, the discharge log, and any incidents yearly, and teach new groups the policy as part of mentoring.

## Common Failures

- Assuming that because the state allows it, the community does too
- A loaded long gun behind the door "for coyotes"
- Keys to the safe left in the lock, or a combination everyone knows
- Open carry at the market stand or at community events, which frightens customers and neighbors
- A pistol set down on a table, a shelf, or the seat of a truck
- Shooting a rifle toward a ridge, road, or house that seemed far enough away
- Shooting a protected predator without checking the law
- Firearms at meetings or hearings where tempers run high
- No plan for temporary off-site storage when someone is in crisis
- A child who knows where the key is hidden

## Money and Legal Tie-In

- Firearm owners buy their own safes. The community budget covers the farm-gun cabinet, training, and ammunition for community-owned farm guns ([Chapter 8](../../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md)).
- Check carry permits, storage law, child-access prevention law, transport law, private-transfer law, hunting and depredation rules, and insurance requirements ([Chapter 4](../04-legal-basics.md))
- Include training completed and any discharges (no private details) in the annual report

> Firearm laws on carrying, storage, transport, transfer, and killing predators differ widely by country, state, county, and city, and they change. This handbook gives no legal advice, so have an attorney review the firearms policy, and check hunting and depredation rules with the local wildlife agency, before any firearm is used on the land.
