# Conflict and Removal

## Conflict: A Ladder
Most conflicts are small if caught early. Use the lowest step that works.
1. **Talk directly.** One calm conversation, with specifics, not accusations.
2. **Ask a neutral member** to help, such as a trained mediator from the group.
3. **Mediation** with two people who are not involved, with agreed outcomes written down.
4. **Board hearing,** if the problem continues or breaks a conduct rule. Both sides are heard, and decisions are in writing. Hearings are closed sessions and are never recorded.
5. **Outside mediation or an attorney,** for serious disputes or legal risk.

Rules for the ladder:
- Train two members in basic mediation, and write down the steps in the charter
- Keep records short, factual, and private
- No decisions made in anger, online, or in front of a crowd
- A person in conflict with a leader has a route that bypasses that leader

## Warnings, Removal, and Fairness
Removal is the last step, not the first, and it must be fair, written, and consistent.
- [ ] A written process: **warning, written agreement to change, review, then removal**
- [ ] The person hears the concern, answers it, and may bring a support person
- [ ] Decision by the board, with reasons in writing and a chance to appeal to an independent member or outside party
- [ ] **Immediate safety exception:** for violence, serious threats, or abuse, separate people at once and call the authorities. Don't wait for a hearing.
- [ ] Have an attorney confirm what tenant, eviction, or notice rules apply before removing anyone from housing, especially where fees are paid ([Chapter 8](../../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md))
- [ ] Never remove someone's belongings, lock them out, or cut utilities without following the legal process
- [ ] Offer help with the transition: a notice period where safe, referrals to local services, and a clear plan for belongings and any money owed
- [ ] Apply the same rules to founders, leaders, and new members
