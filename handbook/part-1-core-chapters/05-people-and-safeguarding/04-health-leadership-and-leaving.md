# Health, Leadership, and Leaving

## Health, Safety, and Emergencies

- [ ] **Trained first-aiders:** at least two, with current certification, and a stocked first-aid kit in every shared building and at the market stand
- [ ] **Emergency contacts** posted: local emergency number, nearest hospital, poison control, fire department, and the site address and directions for responders ([Chapter 12](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/12-roads-and-access.md))
- [ ] A **medical emergency plan:** who calls, who meets the ambulance at the gate, who stays with the person, sized to how long help takes to arrive (see [Remote Medical Plan](#remote-medical-plan))
- [ ] **Overdose and mental-health crises:** know the local crisis line, keep naloxone where legal and trained members to use it, and never leave someone in crisis alone
- [ ] **Illness:** sick people stay out of food handling, harvesting, and packing (Chapters [6](../../part-2-market-engine/06-market-business-basics/README.md) and [7](../../part-2-market-engine/07-production-planning/README.md)), and a sick member's meals and check-ins are arranged
- [ ] **Disasters:** a written plan for storms, high wind, wildfire, fire, flood, and heat or cold waves, with a meeting place, a headcount method, and a go-bag (Chapters [9](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/09-shelter-evolution/README.md) and [16](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/16-wind-power-and-wind-safety.md), [Appendix D15](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d15-disaster-plan-template.md))
- [ ] **Fire drills** and tested smoke and CO detectors in every sleeping area
- [ ] **Medications and private health information** stay private. Share only what is needed in an emergency.
- [ ] Each resident may keep an emergency contact on file, held securely by two named people

## Remote Medical Plan

**Rule:** plan for help being far away. Ask the local ambulance service how long it takes to reach the site, and size the plan to that time. A rural ambulance can take far longer than people expect, and a flood, snowstorm, or fire can cut the road for days.

- [ ] **Ask the response time,** by day, by night, and in bad weather, and record it on the [emergency contact sheet](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d14-emergency-contact-sheet.md)
- [ ] **A way to call for help with no cell signal:** a landline, a satellite messenger, or a two-way radio arranged with a neighbor or the local emergency service
- [ ] **A meeting landmark,** such as the main gate or the nearest public crossroads, where a member waits to guide the ambulance in
- [ ] **Where the response is long** (for example, more than 30 minutes), at least two members trained in **wilderness first aid**, which teaches care for hours, not minutes. Consider a longer wilderness first responder course for one.
- [ ] **A trauma kit** with the first-aid kits: tourniquets, wound packing, splints, and blankets, with members trained to use them
- [ ] **An AED** (a defibrillator that talks the user through each step) in a shared building, with its pads and battery checked monthly, and members trained in CPR
- [ ] **A helicopter landing spot** chosen with the air ambulance service: flat, open, and clear of trees, wires, the wind turbine, and loose items, at the size the service asks for (often about 100 by 100 feet, 30 by 30 meters). Put its GPS coordinates on the emergency contact sheet, keep it mowed or grazed short, and agree how to mark it at night.
- [ ] **If the road is cut for days:** residents keep a supply of their prescription medicines where the pharmacist and prescriber allow, the first-aid supplies are stocked for longer care, and the [disaster plan](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d15-disaster-plan-template.md) names who checks on anyone ill, elders, and young children
- [ ] **Drill it once a year,** including the meeting landmark, the landing spot, and the second way out ([Chapter 12](../../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/12-roads-and-access.md#two-ways-out))

**Tier fit**

- **Tier 0:** the response time asked and recorded, a way to call for help with no cell signal, and a meeting landmark
- **Tier 1:** where the response is long, wilderness first aid for two members, and a trauma kit
- **Tier 2:** an AED in a shared building, and the landing spot agreed with the air ambulance service
- **Tier 3:** the landing spot marked and maintained, and a yearly medical and evacuation drill
- **Tier 4:** review the plan, the response time, and the drills yearly

**Common failures**

- Assuming an ambulance arrives in minutes
- No way to call for help when the cell signal is down
- An AED with a dead battery or expired pads
- A landing spot under power lines, near the turbine, or overgrown
- First-aid training for a short wait, when help is an hour away

## Leadership and Burnout

- Share roles across several people, and rotate where possible
- **Two people for every critical task:** cash, keys, filings, safeguarding, first aid, camera footage, the meeting archive, and each main crop
- Leaders have written duties, term limits, and a handover process (the succession plan in [Chapter 3](../03-charter-and-decision-making.md))
- A named **on-site coordinator** is paid or supported where possible, and gets regular rest
- Hold a short **check-in meeting** each week and a **review** each season: what is working, what hurts
- Watch for exhaustion, resentment, and one person doing everything. Fix the load before it breaks the person.

## Privacy and Records

- Keep members' personal records, complaints, and health details locked and shared only with those who need them
- Record decisions and outcomes, not gossip
- Follow local data and privacy law, and keep records only as long as needed
- Do not post residents' names, children's photos, or schedules publicly

## Leaving Well

- A written **exit process:** notice, return of keys and property, settling any money, and a chance to give feedback
- Members who leave on good terms are the community's best neighbors, so treat leaving as normal
- Ask departing members why they left, and record the lessons in the revision log
