# 4. Legal Basics

**Rules differ by country, state, county, and city, and they change.** This handbook does not give legal advice for any one place. Before you spend money or move anyone onto land, find out what applies where you are.

## Check these first

- **Land use and zoning:** camping, trailers, cabins, greenhouses, assembly buildings (such as the Lodge and the Meeting Hall), how many people may live on the land, and whether rules for housing, RV, or manufactured-home communities apply
- **Building and fire codes:** permits, height limits, egress, occupancy limits, bedroom size, heating, approved shell materials, interior fire-rated finishes, and exit rules for greenhouses
- **Water and septic:** wells, wastewater, and graywater
- **Energy:** permits, height limits, setbacks, noise, and interconnection rules for solar and wind
- **Food sales and storage:** farmers market permits, cottage food and farm rules, sales tax, and whether a public food store counts as a regulated food facility
- **Growing rules:** plant sale or nursery licenses, pesticide use and applicator rules, manure and compost rules for food crops, and any food-safety standards for produce farms
- **Taxes and filings:** sales tax, property tax, income and payroll rules, and annual reports (see [Chapter 8](../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md))
- **Resident fees:** whether a monthly fee counts as rent or lot rent, what tenant rights and eviction rules follow, and fair-housing rules
- **People rules:** background-check and screening law, mandatory reporting of suspected abuse, anti-discrimination law, religious-freedom and belief-based discrimination law, and local rules on weapons and substances (see Chapters [3](03-charter-and-decision-making.md) and [5](05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md))
- **Firearms:** carry permits and where carrying is banned, storage and child-access prevention laws, transport rules, private-transfer and background-check rules, hunting licenses, rules on killing predators and protected species, depredation permits, and what the insurer requires (see [Chapter 5, Firearms Safety](05-people-and-safeguarding/05-firearms-safety.md))
- **Recording and signs:** laws on video and audio recording (many places require consent from everyone recorded, and states differ), notice or sign requirements, privacy of children, how long footage may be kept, and trespass-sign wording (see [Chapter 5](05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md))
- **Meetings and records:** open-meeting or record-keeping rules that apply to the organization type, how long minutes and recordings must or may be kept, and whether recorded meetings are subject to disclosure requests (see [Chapter 9](../part-3-site-and-infrastructure/09-shelter-evolution/README.md))
- **Work expectations:** whether an hours expectation tied to housing could be treated as rent or employment (see [Chapter 5](05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md))
- **Animals:** local limits, vaccination, fencing, waste, and livestock rules, if animals are ever kept
- **Group form and fundraising:** how an association, partnership, or fiscal sponsorship is treated, and what rules apply to collecting contributions (see [Chapter 2](02-forming-a-group.md))
- **Insurance and liability**, including whether crops and greenhouse structures can be covered and what security the insurer expects
- **Nonprofit or land trust formation**, including investment rules, tax exemptions, and how donated assets are reported and taxed

## Where to ask

- The county or city planning and zoning office
- The local building office and fire marshal
- The local health department
- The farmers market manager
- The local agricultural extension service or farm advisor
- The fire department
- Local child and adult protection services, about reporting duties and training
- The local police non-emergency line, about trespass, harassment, and reporting
- A local attorney, especially before signing a lease, buying land, removing a resident, adopting the equal-treatment wording, installing cameras, recording any meeting, or collecting any resident fee
- An accountant, before the first market sale, before the community holds reserves or investments, and before any change in legal structure

## Outside the United States

The handbook was first written with US rules in mind, but its structure works anywhere. Keep every rule and checklist, and swap in the local name for each office, tax, and legal form. Ask an attorney or accountant where you live for the closest match.

| Where the handbook says | Look for the local equivalent of |
|---|---|
| Sales tax | Value-added tax (VAT) or goods and services tax (GST), and its registration threshold |
| County or city planning and zoning office | The local council, municipality, or planning authority |
| Health department | The local food-safety or environmental health authority |
| Agricultural extension service | The government or university farm advisory service |
| Cottage food rules | Home food-business registration rules |
| Nonprofit with tax exemption | A registered charity, association, or nonprofit company, and its tax status |
| Land trust | A community land trust, charitable land company, or cooperative land holding |
| Property tax | Council or municipal tax, rates, or land tax |
| Fair-housing law | Equality, housing, and anti-discrimination law |
| State firearms law | National and regional firearms licensing, storage, and transport law |
| ENERGY STAR or DLC | The national energy-efficiency label |

- Units are given in both US and metric where people will build to them. Use the metric figures and confirm them against the local code.
- Some rules in this handbook are stricter than US law, and some places' laws will be stricter than the handbook. **Always follow whichever is stricter.**
- Where a form or template names a US-specific item, keep the line and rename it.

> When in doubt, ask before you build. Get any approval in writing and keep it in the community files.
