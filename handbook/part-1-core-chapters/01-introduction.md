# 1. Introduction

## What This Handbook Is

This is a **living guide for any group of people willing to try building a community from scratch**: tents or trailers at first, gardens for food, and a stand at the local market to pay the bills. It does not assume money, land, or experience. It assumes honesty, patience, and a willingness to write things down.

The handbook is built to **evolve**. A community starts small, proves each step works, and grows only when its people, records, and money are ready. It is not a promise that every group will succeed. It is a way to lower the odds of the common failures: unclear rules, unsafe sites, unpaid taxes, burned-out leaders, and growth that outruns what the group can carry.

## Who It Is For

- Groups with **little or no funding** who want to start on borrowed, donated, or leased land
- Neighbors, congregations, and mutual aid networks who want to host a pilot
- Supporters who want to help from a distance, and need to know the project can run without them
- Anyone who wants to mentor a new group later

It works **anywhere**. Rules on land, building, food, water, tax, housing, and recording differ by country, state, county, and city, so the handbook tells you what to check, not what your law says. Use your own currency and units. Climate matters too: hot dry regions, cold snowy regions, and windy regions weight the chapters differently.

## The Core Idea: Tiers and Triggers

The community moves through five tiers.

| Tier | Name | In a sentence |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No Funds | A founding group, borrowed land, safe water, a garden, and written agreements |
| 1 | Pilot | A weekly market stand, basic insurance, and records that prove the model |
| 2 | Stabilize | Plumbing, septic, a block food store, fire-safe cabins, secured greenhouses, and steady income |
| 3 | Expand | A nonprofit or land trust, more buildings, a shared Lodge, a Meeting Hall, more growing space, and a stronger board |
| 4 | Own | Buying land, long-term systems, and mentoring new groups |

Each tier ends with an **unlock trigger**: a checklist the community must meet, and then vote on, before it moves up. **Staying at a tier is normal, not failure.** If the community's money falls behind its plan, judged over a full year so that a quiet off-season is never counted as failure, it steps back until it recovers ([Chapter 8](../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/01-budgeting-cash-handling-and-cost-tracking.md)).

## The Principles Behind Every Chapter

1. **Safety first.** Clean water, a legal toilet, safe sleeping spaces, fire-resistant buildings, and emergency plans come before comfort or growth.
2. **Follow the money.** Housing, buildings, and systems follow income, not hope. Pay for growth from earnings and grants, not debt.
3. **Write it down.** Agreements, budgets, logs, and decisions are recorded while everyone still trusts each other.
4. **Two people for every critical job.** Cash, keys, filings, safeguarding, first aid, footage, the archive, and each main crop have a backup.
5. **Fair rules, applied equally.** Founders, leaders, and newcomers follow the same process, whatever anyone believes.
6. **Be a good neighbor.** Pay what is owed, show your records, and welcome questions.
7. **Start small, prove it, then grow.** One season of honest records persuades landowners, donors, and officials better than any plan.
8. **Look after people.** Rest, shared meals, and a kind conflict process keep a community together longer than any building.

## What the Handbook Cannot Do

- It gives **no legal, tax, financial, medical, or engineering advice.** Where a chapter says "ask an attorney, an accountant, an engineer, or the health department," it means it.
- It does not know your climate, soil, or local customs. Adjust it with your agricultural extension service, neighbors, and market manager.
- It cannot replace trust. If the group cannot agree on the basics in [Chapter 2](02-forming-a-group.md), no amount of planning will fix it.
- Its thresholds (months of income, size of reserves, hours of work) are starting points. Your pilot will show which are too easy or too strict.
- It is written for a **village-scale** community (roughly 20-30 residents). A much larger, town-sized community faces extra rules on water systems, wastewater, subdivision, and governance that need their own planning.

## Your First Week

1. **Gather the founding group** of 5-10 committed people ([Chapter 2](02-forming-a-group.md)).
2. **Hold the first meeting** and write a one-paragraph purpose and three to five shared values ([Appendix B2](../appendices/appendix-b-templates/b02-first-meeting-agenda.md)).
3. **Have the honest conversation** about time, money, housing, children, decisions, conflict, leaving, and failure.
4. **Name roles with a backup for each,** and agree how decisions are made.
5. **Open a two-signer community account.**
6. **Book time with an accountant and an attorney,** and ask the market manager about vendor rules.
7. **Set the next three actions,** with names and dates.

## A Note for Supporters Who Cannot Be on Site

If you are funding or encouraging a community from a distance:

- Support the **structure first**: a charter, a nonprofit or land trust, a board with non-resident members, and an on-site coordinator who has backup.
- Give **in ways that don't depend on you**: reserves, equipment, or grants, not promises of monthly top-ups.
- Ask for the **annual report and the tier review** instead of daily updates.
- Never take **title, keys, or sole control** of money. The community should be able to run, and survive, without you.

## How This Handbook Grows

Every chapter ends with common failures, and every tier ends with lessons. When your community learns something the book missed, **add it to the [revision log](../../REVISION_LOG.md)** and share it with the next group you mentor. That is how this handbook stays alive.

> This handbook is a starting point, not a guarantee. Rules, risks, and conditions vary widely by location. Confirm anything legal, financial, medical, or structural with a qualified local professional before you act.
