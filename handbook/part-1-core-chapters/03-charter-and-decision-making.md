# 3. Charter and Decision-Making

## Standing Rules
- The founding agreement is the first draft of the charter. An attorney reviews it before Tier 1, and it states what happens to money and property if the group dissolves.
- Everyone is treated equally, whatever they believe or don't believe. The community has no official faith, ideology, or party. No building is dedicated to a single belief, and the Lodge and the Meeting Hall are open to all on equal terms, with booking calendars and a monthly cap per group.
- All lighting is LED, and daylight comes first where a roof allows.
- Garden spiders are not killed.
- Heat-wave and cold-snap plans are part of the charter, along with high-wind and dust-storm plans.
- No cabin is taller than two stories plus a short attic, and no cabin houses more than four residents.
- Every cabin has a noncombustible shell (steel at minimum, or filled concrete block) and a fire-rated interior finish, such as drywall, where code and design allow.
- Any dirt road on the property is upgraded to gravel as funds allow.
- Safe drinking water and a legal toilet are required from Tier 0.
- No drain is connected to a cabin until the septic system is permitted, installed, and inspected.
- Community food storage is filled concrete block, keys are logged, and stock is inspected monthly.
- Greenhouses lock from the inside with a no-key exit, are secured from outside only after a check that they are empty, and no one is ever locked in.
- Cameras follow the written Cameras and Surveillance Policy: visible orange cameras with black-and-yellow signs, shared and outdoor areas only, no audio unless an attorney confirms it is lawful, two custodians, logged access, and a retention limit.
- Open meetings in the Meeting Hall are recorded for the historical archive under the written meeting recording policy, with written minutes as the official record. Closed sessions are never recorded.
- Market cash is counted by two people at the start and end of every market day, and every sale is logged.
- Spending limits and approval levels are set in the charter, and the budget is reviewed against actuals every month.
- No broad-spectrum pesticides are used in or near gardens, and every spray is logged.
- Conduct rules, the conflict ladder, the removal and appeal process, safeguarding rules, and the weapons and substances policies are written into the charter. No one is removed without the written process, except in an immediate safety emergency.
- Outside harassment follows the written response plan: calm, documented, and never retaliatory, with one named spokesperson.
- Each adult member is asked to give about 12 hours of work a week, adjusted fairly in writing. Hours are a guideline and are never a condition of housing.
- Animals are optional and start at Tier 3 or later. Keeping animals needs a group vote, and animals that are kept are fed, watered, and checked every day.
- No wind turbine is installed without a year of wind data, engineer sign-off, and permits.
- The operating reserve is never converted. Any store-of-value holding needs a capped share, a board vote including non-residents, two-person custody, and a signed inventory.
- Every tax, fee, and filing deadline is on a compliance calendar with two responsible members. No exemption is claimed unless it has been granted.
- An annual report is published and shared on request.
- Any resident fee is set per planned lot, written down, applied the same way to everyone, and paid into a dedicated property tax fund. Only residents pay it, and donations are never counted on for the tax bill.
- Land is bought only after due diligence, a cooling-off period, a supermajority vote (threshold set here), and board approval.
- Title to all land is held by the nonprofit or land trust, never by an individual member.

## Equal Treatment and Shared Space

**Rule:** **Everyone is treated equally, whatever they believe or don't believe.** The community is neutral. It has no official faith, ideology, or party, and no one is favored or excluded for theirs.

**Buildings**
- **No building is dedicated to a single belief, faith, or political cause.** This applies to every belief equally, including non-religious ones.
- **Two shared halls,** the Community Lodge for social and general use and the Meeting Hall for governance meetings (Chapter 9), serve everyone on equal terms
- Each hall is **booked on equal terms**, first come first served, with the same rules and costs for every group
- Nothing permanent is displayed in shared spaces that favors one belief. Personal items belong in private spaces.
- Residents may hold quiet worship or gatherings **in their own homes,** or in the Lodge by booking, within the conduct and quiet-hours rules

**Daily life**
- No religious or political tests for joining, housing, work, or fees
- No required participation in anyone's ceremonies, prayers, or events
- No promoting beliefs during shared work, at the market stand, or to visitors
- Reasonable accommodation for dress, diet, rest days, and holidays, applied fairly
- Disputes over belief follow the same conflict ladder as any other (Chapter 5)

**Legal check (before adopting)**
- [ ] Have an attorney confirm that the wording is neutral and complies with fair-housing, religious-freedom, and anti-discrimination law where the community is located
- [ ] Use content-neutral land-use language, such as "no single-purpose assembly buildings," not a ban on any one faith
- [ ] Confirm what local zoning allows for assembly uses, and whether a shared hall needs a particular permit
- [ ] Do not restrict private prayer, religious items, or gatherings inside homes

**Common failures**
- A rule aimed at one faith, which invites legal challenge
- Banning private worship or religious items in homes
- A "neutral" hall that in practice is booked mostly by one group
- Treating non-religious beliefs as exempt from the rule
- Founders exempting their own ceremonies

> No wording fully prevents belief-based disputes, and a court could still scrutinize it. Deed restrictions or an existing landowner's terms may add rules, so the attorney step matters most here.
