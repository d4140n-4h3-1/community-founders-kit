# 16. Wind Power and Wind Safety

## Principle

**Measure first, then decide.** Wind is a tool for cutting power costs and filling solar's gaps, and it is also a hazard to every structure on the site. Treat both sides: what wind can give, and what it can break. Wind resources vary widely by site, and small turbines are often poor value where wind is average or turbulent.

## Part A: Wind as Power (Tier 3 and up, funding permitting)

**Honest fit check**

- **Strengths:** works at night and in winter, pairs well with solar, and can charge a heat store such as the sand battery ([Chapter 11](11-sand-battery-heating.md))
- **Limits:** output rises steeply with wind speed, so a mediocre site gives poor returns. Turbulence from trees and buildings cuts output and wears out machines. Moving parts need upkeep.
- **Compared to solar:** solar is usually cheaper, simpler, and easier to permit at small scale. Wind makes sense mainly where the site is windy and open.

**Site assessment (before any purchase)**

- [ ] Check public wind-resource maps, then **measure on site** with an anemometer at planned hub height for at least a full year if possible (log in [Appendix A15](../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a15-wind-site-assessment.md))
- [ ] Rough rule of thumb: an average of about 10 mph (4.5 m/s) or more at hub height is the usual minimum for small wind to be worthwhile. Confirm with an installer.
- [ ] Put the tower well above obstacles. A common guide is at least 30 ft (9 m) above anything within about 300 ft (90 m).
- [ ] Keep it away from cabins, roads, the septic field, and property lines by at least the tower height plus a margin
- [ ] Check wildlife and flight-path rules, because bird and bat risk and aviation lighting can apply

**Rules**

- [ ] **Separate the tower from living areas.** Never mount a turbine on a cabin or greenhouse.
- [ ] Use a **certified turbine** with an engineer-approved tower and foundation, installed by a qualified installer
- [ ] **Permits first:** zoning, height limits, setbacks, noise, building and electrical permits, and any utility interconnection rules
- [ ] Add an **automatic shutdown** for over-speed, and a manual stop
- [ ] Fence the tower base, and post warning signs. Keep children away ([Chapter 5](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md)).
- [ ] Inspect yearly (bolts, guy wires, blades, bearings), and log it ([Appendix A16](../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a16-turbine-inspection-log.md))
- [ ] Count noise, vibration, and shadow flicker against neighbors, and talk to them early

**How wind fits with the rest of the power plan**

- Lower loads first: LEDs and solar tubes ([Chapter 14](14-lighting-standard.md)), insulation, and mini-splits ([Chapter 9](09-shelter-evolution/README.md))
- Use **hybrid solar plus wind with batteries** for homes and shared buildings, sized by an electrician or engineer
- Surplus can charge the **sand battery** ([Chapter 11](11-sand-battery-heating.md)), which needs solar or wind records before it is built
- At larger scale, **community wind** or a shared microgrid may need utility, state, or public-utility approval

**Tier fit**

- **Tier 0-2:** no turbines. Install a small **anemometer** at Tier 1 and log wind data, and use solar and LED first.
- **Tier 3:** if a year of data supports it, a pilot turbine for a shared building, greenhouse, or heat store, with engineer sign-off
- **Tier 4:** add capacity only if production records justify it. Review yearly.

## Part B: Wind as a Hazard (all tiers)

Wind damages light structures first, and it spreads wildfire.

- [ ] **Anchor everything:** canopies, hoop houses, greenhouses, tents, sheds, and cabins ([Chapter 9](09-shelter-evolution/README.md)). Use weights, ground anchors, and engineered tie-downs, not stakes alone.
- [ ] **Market canopy weights** are not optional ([Chapter 6](../part-2-market-engine/06-market-business-basics/README.md)). Take canopies down in high wind.
- [ ] **Greenhouse covers:** orient and brace for the prevailing wind, and add windbreaks ([Chapter 7](../part-2-market-engine/07-production-planning/README.md))
- [ ] **Windbreaks:** rows of trees or shrubs, set far enough from buildings to avoid fire spread and falling limbs
- [ ] **Dust and drought:** in dry, windy areas, use cover crops and mulch to hold soil ([Chapter 7](../part-2-market-engine/07-production-planning/README.md))
- [ ] **Wildfire:** keep defensible space around buildings, clear dry brush, and add wind to the disaster plan ([Chapter 5](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md), [Appendix D15](../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d15-disaster-plan-template.md))
- [ ] **Wildfire-ready buildings:** a noncombustible shell, noncombustible roofing, ember-resistant vents, and drywall-protected interiors ([Chapter 9](09-shelter-evolution/README.md))
- [ ] **Wind-driven snow:** plan drifts, roof loads, and road clearing where snow falls ([Chapter 12](12-roads-and-access.md))
- [ ] **Extreme weather plans:** add high-wind and dust-storm response to heat and cold plans ([Chapter 9](09-shelter-evolution/README.md))

## Common Failures

- Buying a turbine with no wind data, then getting little power
- Placing it too low, or beside trees and buildings
- Mounting it on a roof, causing vibration and structural damage
- Skipping permits, then being ordered to take it down
- No maintenance, so blades or bolts fail
- Unanchored canopies and greenhouses that become flying debris
- Windbreak trees planted so close they add fire risk
- Standard soffit vents that let wildfire embers into the attic

> Wind resources, height limits, noise rules, and interconnection rules vary by site and by jurisdiction. Have a qualified installer and the local permitting office confirm what applies before you buy anything.
