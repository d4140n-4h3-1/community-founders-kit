# Greenhouse Security

**Rule:** **Every greenhouse door can be locked from the inside, and nobody can ever be trapped inside it.** The inside lock is for people working in the greenhouse. When the greenhouse is empty, it is secured from the outside with a keyed lock on the key log. Locks never block escape.

**Locks and doors**
- [ ] **Inside lock:** a thumb-turn or slide bolt that opens with one hand and no key, so anyone inside can always get out
- [ ] **Outside lock:** a keyed padlock or deadbolt, used only when the greenhouse is empty, with the keys on the key log (see Food Storage Building)
- [ ] **Never lock someone in:** the outside lock is closed only after a check that the greenhouse is empty, and a second person confirms at dusk
- [ ] **Two exits** in any greenhouse large enough to need them, with an exit that opens outward and is never blocked by benches or stock
- [ ] **Sturdy frame and hinges:** steel door and frame, with hinges inside or pinned so they cannot be lifted off
- [ ] **Panic hardware** on larger buildings, and no interior-only lock on a door anyone could be shut behind

**Glazing, vents, and edges**
- [ ] **Glazing:** polycarbonate panels resist breaking far better than glass or thin film, and are cheaper to repair
- [ ] **Film-covered hoop houses** cannot be truly secured, so keep valuables, tools, and stock out of them overnight
- [ ] **Vents and fans:** louvers and roof vents get metal mesh or bars sized so nothing can be reached through them, while still letting air flow
- [ ] **Base and foundation:** a low wall or buried edge, so no one can lift or crawl under the panels
- [ ] **No easy climbing:** keep trees, stacks, and sheds away from the roof edge

**Lighting, visibility, and alerts**
- [ ] **LED motion-sensor light** at the door, aimed down and shielded (Chapter 14)
- [ ] **Placed where it is seen** from cabins and the main path, with clear lines of sight
- [ ] **A visible orange camera and black-and-yellow sign** at the door, under the Cameras and Surveillance Policy (Chapter 5)
- [ ] **A simple door or glass-break alarm,** battery or solar powered, if the budget allows

**What goes inside, and what does not**
- [ ] **Tools, chemicals, and fuel are not stored in the greenhouse** (Chapter 7)
- [ ] **Seedlings and plant stock** are the main target, so keep a count in the harvest or sales log
- [ ] **No cash or card reader** is left in the greenhouse
- [ ] **Heaters and grow lights** follow the fire rules and are switched off when the greenhouse is empty, unless a thermostat protects crops from freezing

**Safety cautions**
- Greenhouses get **very hot and humid.** A person locked in on a summer day can be in danger within an hour or two, so never lock anyone inside, and check on anyone working alone.
- Check **CO and heat** where fuel heaters are used, and keep vents working
- Every worker knows the **emergency exit,** and it is tested each season
- Children and visitors do not enter alone (Chapter 5)

**Key control**
- A written list of key holders and a sign-out log, as for the food store
- Change locks when a key holder leaves
- Two members hold the keys for each greenhouse, so access never depends on one person

**Tier fit**
- **Tier 1:** the hoop house has a closing door with a simple outside hasp, and valuables are removed overnight
- **Tier 2:** the seedling greenhouse gets the inside lock, the outside keyed lock, polycarbonate glazing where practical, mesh on the vents, a motion light, and a key log
- **Tier 3:** multiple greenhouses, a door or glass-break alarm, and cameras under the written policy
- **Tier 4:** review the greenhouse security log yearly with the food-store audit

**Common failures**
- A lock that can only be opened from outside, or that needs a key to get out
- Locking a door with someone still inside
- Thin film or glass that anyone can cut, with valuable stock inside
- Large vents with no mesh
- Tools and chemicals stored in the greenhouse
- Keys copied freely and not logged
- Trees or stacks that let someone climb onto the roof
- Counting on the lock and camera to protect crops from heat, frost, or power failure

> Fire, exit, and occupancy rules for greenhouses vary by location. Confirm what applies to your buildings with the local building office or fire marshal.
