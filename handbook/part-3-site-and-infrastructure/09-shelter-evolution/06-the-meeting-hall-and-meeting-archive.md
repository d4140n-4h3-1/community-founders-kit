# The Meeting Hall and Meeting Archive

**Rule:** **The community has one designated Meeting Hall for governance meetings, and an open meeting there is recorded for the historical archive.** The recording supplements the written minutes and never replaces them. The hall serves the community's decisions, not any belief, faith, party, or club, so it fits the equal-treatment rule ([Chapter 3](../../part-1-core-chapters/03-charter-and-decision-making.md)). The Community Lodge stays the general-purpose social hall and has no cameras.

**What the Meeting Hall is for**
- Monthly community meetings and the seasonal review
- Board meetings, budget and tier-review votes, and land or charter decisions
- Open days and public hearings, where neighbors and officials may attend
- It is not for social events, private ceremonies, or conflict hearings. Those use the Lodge or a private room.

**Design**
- [ ] **A separate small building with a steel or block shell and a fire-rated interior,** like any assembly building, with a posted occupancy limit, two exits, detectors, extinguishers, and an emergency light. A separate building keeps the Lodge camera-free.
- [ ] **Fixed seating plan** (tables in a horseshoe or rows) and a clear speaking position, so everyone can be seen and heard
- [ ] **Accessible entry, restrooms, and seating,** with space for wheelchairs
- [ ] **Good acoustics:** soft surfaces or panels, and a small, simple microphone setup
- [ ] **Solar tube and LED lighting** ([Chapter 14](../14-lighting-standard.md))
- [ ] **One dedicated recording camera and microphone** in a vandal-resistant housing, aimed at the speaking area and table, and never at the door, windows, or any seating for children
- [ ] **A "RECORDING IN PROGRESS" indicator** (a light that comes on only while recording) and a black-and-yellow sign at the entrance
- [ ] **A locked equipment cabinet** for the recorder and storage drive, with two key holders
- [ ] **A quiet seating area outside** for anyone who steps out of a recorded session
- [ ] **No permanent shrines, symbols, or banners** that favor any belief

**What is recorded and what is not**
*Recorded (open meetings)*
- Community and board meetings, votes, budget and tier reviews, and public hearings

*Never recorded (closed sessions)*
- Conflict-ladder hearings, removal and appeal hearings, and safeguarding matters
- Personal, health, or family matters, and complaints about named individuals
- Legal advice, and any meeting where a minor's private information is discussed
- Anything a person asks to take off the record for privacy or safety reasons, within the written limits

Closed sessions are announced, the recording is paused and then restarted, and the minutes note the pause and the legal basis (not the content).

**Consent and notice**
- [ ] **Every adult member agrees in the membership agreement** that open meetings are recorded for the archive ([Appendix B4](../../appendices/appendix-b-templates/b04-membership-agreement.md))
- [ ] **A sign at the entrance and an announcement at the start of every meeting:** "This meeting is being recorded for the community archive."
- [ ] **Visitors and guests are told** at the door and in the visitor welcome sheet, and may sit in an unrecorded area or leave
- [ ] **Children are not recorded:** minors attend only where the camera cannot capture them, or the meeting is held without them
- [ ] **Audio is lawful only after an attorney review** of local consent rules (some states need consent from everyone recorded, others from one party), with the board's written approval
- [ ] **Speakers may decline to be recorded** by using a clearly marked unrecorded seat or written comment, so no one is forced to choose between speaking and privacy

**The archive**
- [ ] **Official record first:** the written minutes are the legal record, taken by a named secretary and a backup. The recording is a supporting historical resource.
- [ ] **Naming and storage:** each recording is saved by date, with an index entry (date, type, who attended, topics, votes, [Appendix D18](../../appendices/appendix-d-logs-sheets-and-reports/d18-meeting-archive-index.md)), on an off-site or cloud backup and a second offline copy
- [ ] **Two archivists,** named in the policy, who hold the keys to the equipment and the storage
- [ ] **Retention:** recordings of open meetings are kept as a permanent historical archive, unless the board votes otherwise under a written schedule. This differs from the 30-day security-camera limit ([Chapter 5](../../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md)).
- [ ] **Redaction:** names, addresses, and private details are removed from copies shared outside the community, and closed-session material is never in the archive
- [ ] **Access:** residents may view the archive on request, with a log. Public release of selected recordings needs board approval. The archive is for members, not public posting, unless the board votes otherwise.
- [ ] **No editing of the original,** only labeled excerpts, and no one may alter or delete a recording except under the retention schedule
- [ ] **Errors and objections:** a person may ask for a correction note or review of a segment, handled by the board under the conflict ladder

**Rules for the camera and recorder**
- [ ] Records only while a meeting is in session, and shuts off when the meeting ends
- [ ] Never used for monitoring residents between meetings
- [ ] Never used as evidence in a personal dispute unless the board approves under the camera policy
- [ ] Meeting Hall footage is kept separate from security footage, and the two never share storage or custodians
- [ ] Inspect the camera, microphone, indicator light, sign, and backups monthly with the camera check ([Appendix A19](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a19-camera-and-sign-inspection.md)), and follow the meeting checklist ([Appendix A20](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a20-meeting-recording-checklist.md))

**Fit with the rest of the handbook**
- **[Chapter 3](../../part-1-core-chapters/03-charter-and-decision-making.md) (Charter):** the Meeting Hall is open on equal terms, and booking and agenda rules are the same for everyone
- **[Chapter 5](../../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md) (Cameras and Surveillance Policy):** this section is a named exception for audio and the longer retention, and every other camera rule still applies
- **[Chapter 2](../../part-1-core-chapters/02-forming-a-group.md) (How the Group Decides):** minutes of every meeting are still required, and votes are recorded in writing
- **[Chapter 8](../../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md):** the annual report notes the number of recorded meetings and archive costs, never content
- **Tier 3 and Tier 4:** the archive supports the tier review, mentoring new groups, and succession, so lessons are not lost when founders leave

**Tier fit**
- **Tier 1-2:** meetings use the covered shared space, with written minutes and no recording until the policy is adopted
- **Tier 2:** adopt the recording policy ([Appendix B10](../../appendices/appendix-b-templates/b10-meeting-recording-policy-template.md)), get an attorney's review, and add consent to the membership agreement
- **Tier 3:** build or designate the Meeting Hall, install the camera, microphone, indicator, and sign, name two archivists, and set up off-site backup
- **Tier 4:** review the archive, the retention schedule, and any objections yearly, and use it to mentor new groups

**Common failures**
- Recording audio before checking consent law, or without notice
- Recording closed sessions, or hearings about named people
- Capturing children on camera
- Treating the recording as the official record and skipping minutes
- One person controlling the archive, with no log
- Mixing meeting footage with security footage
- Editing or deleting recordings to settle a dispute
- Public posting of full recordings with names and private details
- Using the hall for social events or private ceremonies, so its purpose blurs
- Letting the recording chill discussion, so people stop speaking honestly

> Recording, consent, open-meeting, retention, and privacy rules vary by state and by organization type. Confirm with an attorney before any meeting is recorded, especially audio, and before any recording is shared outside the community.
