# Interior Fire Protection

**Rule:** **Drywall, or an equivalent fire-rated interior finish, is used wherever the code and the building allow.** The shell is noncombustible (steel or block), and the inside is protected so that insulation, framing, and contents are slower to catch and spread fire. Drywall slows fire spread, it does not stop it, and it only works while it stays intact and sealed.

**Where it goes**
- **Interior walls and ceilings of steel cabins,** over the framing and insulation, so insulation is never left exposed
- **Block cabins:** drywall on furring over the block, in living and sleeping areas, hallways, and kitchens, so every cabin has the same finished, fire-protected interior
- **Ceilings and walls shared with mechanical spaces,** such as the attic hatch area and the heater or air-handler closet
- **Around heat sources,** such as stoves, flues, and heaters, using the clearances and noncombustible backing the manufacturer requires
- **Between sleeping rooms and the kitchen** in family cabins
- **Storage rooms** that hold fuel, tools, or batteries
- **The Lodge, the Meeting Hall, the shared kitchen, and the bathhouse,** as the fire code requires

**Choosing the board**
- [ ] **Type X (fire-rated)** for walls and ceilings around sleeping areas, heat sources, and shared walls, where the local code calls for it
- [ ] **Moisture-resistant board or cement board** in bathrooms, kitchens, laundry, and any damp area. Standard drywall fails when wet.
- [ ] **Cement board or other noncombustible backing** behind stoves and wherever the manufacturer requires
- [ ] **Thickness and layers** as the code or the engineer specifies, since a rating depends on the whole assembly, not the board alone
- [ ] Confirm that the assembly **matches a tested, listed rating** if the local office requires one

**Installing it**
- [ ] **Seal gaps and penetrations** around pipes, wiring, vents, and light fixtures with fire-rated sealant or collars, so fire and smoke can't travel through
- [ ] **Tape and finish the joints,** since open joints weaken the barrier
- [ ] **Use the right fasteners** into steel studs or furring, at the spacing the rating requires
- [ ] **Keep it dry,** and fix leaks quickly, since wet drywall softens and grows mold
- [ ] **Protect recessed lights and solar tube openings** with rated housings or sealed collars where they pass through a rated ceiling (Chapter 14)
- [ ] **Do not cover** required access panels, shutoffs, or detectors
- [ ] **Hire a qualified installer** or follow a trained crew's method for any rated assembly

**Drywall in block cabins**
- [ ] **Furring strips or steel channels** fastened to the block with anchors suited to masonry, spaced to the board manufacturer's and code requirements
- [ ] **Run conduit and plumbing in the furring cavity** (or in the block before grouting), so finished walls stay clean and nothing is cut into reinforced block later
- [ ] **Leave a small air gap or use insulation in the cavity** as the design requires, and never trap moisture between the block and the board
- [ ] **Damp-proof the exterior and drain the foundation first,** then check for moisture before closing the walls
- [ ] **Insulate on the outside of the block where possible,** so the block stays inside the thermal envelope and still evens out temperature swings. If insulation must go inside, use the thinnest assembly the climate allows.
- [ ] Leave one or two block walls exposed only if the design and fire code allow, such as a utility closet or the porch
- [ ] Bathrooms and laundry get cement board or moisture-resistant board, finished with tile or a washable surface

**How it fits with the rest of the cabin**
- **Insulation:** spray foam and rigid foam are combustible, so cover them with an approved thermal barrier, usually drywall
- **Solar tubes and the attic:** keep tube sections out of fire-rated walls and ceilings, or use rated collars, as the code allows (Chapter 14)
- **Heating:** drywall does not replace clearances or vented heaters
- **Wildfire:** drywall protects the inside, but ember-resistant vents, noncombustible roofs, and defensible space protect the outside (Chapter 16)

**Tier fit**
- **Tier 2:** the first cabins get drywall on walls and ceilings, with Type X or cement board near heat sources
- **Tier 2-3:** every new block cabin is framed and finished inside as part of the build, not left bare
- **Tier 2-3:** the shared kitchen (Tier 2), and the Lodge, Meeting Hall, and bathhouse (Tier 3), follow the fire-rating rules for assembly and wet areas as each is built
- **Tier 4:** inspect and repair the interior fire barriers during the yearly safety review (Appendix A17)

**Common failures**
- Leaving spray foam or rigid foam exposed with no thermal barrier
- Using standard drywall in damp rooms, so it rots
- Cutting big holes for lights, vents, or pipes and not sealing them
- Open joints, missing screws, or damaged board that were never repaired
- Treating drywall as a substitute for detectors, exits, or heater clearances
- Covering a rated ceiling with a solar tube or fixture that breaks the rating
- Gluing or screwing board directly to damp block, so moisture rots it
- Chasing conduit and pipe channels into filled block after the fact

**Inspection**
- Check walls and ceilings each season for holes, water stains, soft spots, and unsealed penetrations
- Log repairs, and keep any inspection reports in the community files

> Fire ratings depend on the full assembly and on local code. Confirm which boards, thicknesses, furring, and finishes apply with the local building office or fire marshal before you build.
