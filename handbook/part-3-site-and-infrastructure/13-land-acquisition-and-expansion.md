# 13. Land Acquisition and Expansion

## Rule

**The community buys land only after it votes, completes due diligence, and confirms it can pay for upkeep.** A willing seller does not override the unlock triggers. Title is held by the nonprofit or land trust, never by an individual member.

## Two Kinds of Expansion

- **First purchase** (leased land to owned land): Tier 3 → 4
- **Adjacent or additional land:** any time after Tier 3, with the same checklist

## Before the Seller Is Ready

- [ ] Keep a standing "land wish list": acreage range, budget ceiling, must-haves (water, road access, soil, zoning)
- [ ] Build a relationship with neighbors and the current landowner
- [ ] Ask for a **right of first refusal** or purchase option in the lease, so the community gets the first chance to buy. This is required before any Tier 2 building on leased land ([Appendix B7](../appendices/appendix-b-templates/b07-right-of-first-refusal.md))
- [ ] Keep the legal entity (nonprofit or trust) in good standing, since sellers and lenders check it

## Due Diligence (in order, tracking sheet in Appendix A7)

1. **Attorney review** of the offer and all documents
2. **Title search and title insurance:** confirm the seller owns it and nothing is attached to it
3. **Survey:** boundaries, easements, encroachments
4. **Zoning and local rules:** camping, cabins, greenhouses, assembly buildings, approved building materials, septic, market sales, and any wind or solar limits
5. **Water:** well records, test results, water rights, or hookup availability
6. **Soil and percolation test:** this decides whether septic is possible, and a soil test for growing food ([Chapter 7](../part-2-market-engine/07-production-planning/README.md))
7. **Floodplain and drainage maps**
8. **Road and emergency access:** legal access to a public road, not just a handshake
9. **Mineral, water, wind, and timber rights:** confirm what transfers with the land
10. **Environmental check:** old dumps, fuel tanks, contamination
11. **Property tax:** the amount due, and whether any exemption applies to the community or carries over from the seller (confirm in writing before closing)
12. **Deed restrictions:** any covenants that limit buildings, uses, or assembly spaces

## Capacity Check (before saying yes)

- [ ] Septic, water, and roads can serve the larger population, with a plan for each
- [ ] Insurance covers the added land and structures
- [ ] Annual costs (taxes, insurance, road upkeep, utilities) fit the budget with the reserve intact, assuming no exemption unless one is confirmed
- [ ] If the exemption is not confirmed, the one-year property tax fund and the fee policy are ready ([Chapter 8](../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md))
- [ ] Enough members, with the leadership and safeguarding capacity ([Chapter 5](../part-1-core-chapters/05-people-and-safeguarding/README.md)), to maintain it. Land without people becomes a liability.

## Paying for It

- **Preferred:** cash from reserves, grants, and donations
- **Acceptable with limits:** seller financing with a fair rate, no balloon payment, and no personal guarantees from members
- **Avoid:** bank debt that market income cannot cover through a bad season
- **Rule of thumb:** the payment should be coverable by income after a poor harvest year
- Never use member homes or personal assets as collateral
- Land is bought from cash, grants, and donations, not by converting the operating reserve ([Chapter 8](../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md))

## Decision Process

1. Present the opportunity, due diligence results, and full cost to the whole group
2. Allow a cooling-off period before voting
3. Require a supermajority (threshold set in the charter)
4. Record the vote and reasons in the revision log
5. Have the board, including non-residents, approve before signing

## Phased Use

- Do not develop all of the new land at once
- **Year 1:** survey, soil test, cover crops, and fence
- **Year 2:** roads, water, and gardens
- **Year 3 onward:** housing and septic, if the checklists are met
- Keep the new land at the tier its infrastructure supports

## If the Seller Wants to Move Fast

Say so plainly: "We need time for diligence and a group vote." Walk away from any deal that cannot wait for these steps. A deal that disappears because of due diligence was a deal the community should not have made.

## Common Failures

- Buying on a handshake before the survey and title are done
- Taking on a payment that one bad season can't cover
- Buying land with no legal road access
- Learning after closing that the soil fails the percolation test
- Expanding the footprint faster than the membership can maintain it
- Putting title in one person's name
- Skipping the group vote because "the deal won't last"
- Budgeting for a property tax exemption that is never granted
- Missing a deed restriction that bans the buildings you planned

> Contract, title, and septic rules differ by location, so the attorney step matters more here than anywhere else in the book.
