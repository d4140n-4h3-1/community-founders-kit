# Wash-and-Pack, Sources, and Hot Water

## Wash-and-Pack Station
Market produce is a food business, so this station needs clean water above all else.
- [ ] Potable water only, tested
- [ ] Separate sinks: one for washing produce, one for hands
- [ ] Drain to an approved system, not the ground
- [ ] Hot water if local food rules require it
- [ ] Smooth, cleanable surfaces and a floor that drains
- [ ] Check local cottage food and farm rules for what the inspector expects

## Water Sources
| Source | Good for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Public or rural water hookup | Reliable, tested by the utility | Connection fees, line distance |
| Well | Long-term, low running cost | Drilling cost, testing, pump power, water-rights rules |
| Hauled water | Tier 0-1 | Cost per trip, container hygiene |
| Rainwater | Gardens, greenhouses | Not for drinking unless treated and tested, and some places limit collection |

## Cold-Weather Protection (where freezing occurs)
In cold climates, hard freezes are the most common cause of burst pipes.
- [ ] Bury lines below the frost line, or insulate and heat-trace exposed pipes
- [ ] Frost-proof hose bibs; drain and disconnect hoses before a freeze
- [ ] Insulate every above-ground pipe and the pump housing
- [ ] Know the main shutoff location and post it in each building
- [ ] Cold-snap plan includes dripping faucets and a stored water reserve

## Hot Water
- **Tier 2:** small electric or propane tank heater at the kitchen and wash station
- **Tier 3:** tankless or heat pump water heater, sized to need
- **Tier 4:** solar thermal or solar-assisted electric
- Set heaters to about 120°F (49°C) and fit a pressure relief valve
