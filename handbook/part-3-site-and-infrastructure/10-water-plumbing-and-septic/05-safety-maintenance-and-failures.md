# Safety, Maintenance, and Failures

## Cross-Connection and Safety
- [ ] Backflow preventer on any line that touches both garden and drinking water
- [ ] Lead-free, potable-rated pipes and fittings only
- [ ] Drinking-water hoses are food-grade (white or blue)
- [ ] Hand-wash signs at toilets and the wash station
- [ ] Shutoff valves at each building and appliance

## Maintenance Schedule
- **Monthly:** check for leaks, clean containers and filters, test drip lines, and check the pump house lock
- **Each fall:** winterize hoses, insulate pipes, drain outdoor lines (where freezing occurs, [Appendix A6](../../appendices/appendix-a-checklists/a06-winterizing.md))
- **Each spring:** flush lines, check the pump, review the septic schedule
- **Yearly:** water test, backflow test, and update the log

## Common Failures
- Building cabin toilets before the septic is permitted
- Sizing septic for today's headcount or for fewer bedrooms than will be used, or skipping the soil test in clay
- Driving over the tank or field, or planting trees on it
- Letting the tank go unpumped for a decade
- Using garden hoses or unrated containers for drinking water
- Skipping the water test, or treating rainwater as drinkable
- No freeze protection, so one cold snap bursts the lines
- Sending wash-and-pack water to the ground
- Unfilled block trench walls collapsing, or trenches with no drainage
- Mixing gas or power with water lines
- An unlocked pump house or shutoff that anyone can tamper with

## Money Tie-In
- Track water, hauling, and septic costs in the expense log
- Water and septic fees go on the compliance calendar ([Chapter 8](../../part-2-market-engine/08-money-rules/README.md))
- Plumbing is a major Tier 2 cost, so spread it over the tier and do the wash-and-pack station first, since it earns the income

> Water and wastewater rules are set locally, and a failed inspection can shut down the whole community. A system serving many people or connections may be regulated as a public water system. Confirm requirements for wells, septic, graywater, and food-contact water before spending any money.
